# Feature Specification: Longer engagements (6 to 48 hours)

**Feature Branch**: `023-long-engagements` (developed on `ccr-fd0a0c59-4nxg4k`)

**Created**: 2026-11-17

**Status**: Implemented (an investigation, not a gate; nothing trained; no GPU)

**Input**: Owner, issue #42: "Let's consider applying our TMA to much longer engagements, such as
6, 12, 24 and 48 hour periods. Consider the implementation challenges in doing this. Include in
the Viewer and Time-Late pages."

**SRD traceability**: §1 (scope: bearings-only TMA of one target; the browser viewer); §7 (the
site). No gate criterion is touched and no registered record is re-read. Annex B's manoeuvre
statistics are used unchanged for the target.

## Background

Every engagement so far is 40 to 120 minutes long (MAN2, generator 0.6.0) with one ownship zig.
The standing systems are built for that:

- the 0.6.0 kernel's manoeuvre table holds 32 changes (`sim/scenario.py` `MAX_TM`), and its
  ownship makes one zig (and an optional second);
- the real-time model E's exported graph has a fixed 720-row window (2 hours at 10 s);
- C, C″'s zig veto and the IMM's start read a single `zig_index`;
- the time-late control `ctl` searches a period's manoeuvre history one change at a time with at
  most ten manoeuvres, refitting the whole period at each step.

A 48-hour engagement holds about 100 target manoeuvres and 90 ownship legs.

## Decisions taken (AGENTS.md: decide and record)

- **An investigation, not a gate.** No criterion, no decision record, no registered result. The
  numbers are a feasibility measurement on four engagements and are labelled as such. Alternatives
  considered: a registered study with tens of engagements per duration (days of CPU; premature
  before the systems can run on long engagements at all).
- **A separate simulator (`src/tma2/long/sim.py`)**, not a change to the 0.6.0 kernel, whose
  output must stay byte-identical. It uses the project's PRNG (`derive_seed`) and Annex B's target
  statistics (`configs/timelate/tl-ranges.json`); the ownship runs 20–40 minute legs with a turn
  at each and keeps the target between 3 and 25 km, steering on the *true* target position (a
  simplification: a real ownship steers on its own solution). Fresh seed **348**; one engagement
  per duration (indices 0–3: 6, 12, 24, 48 hours). 5-second bearings, σ = 1°, dropouts 0–10%.
- **Windows, not whole-engagement solves.** Each standing system runs on windows of the size it
  was built and decided on: real-time on the last 2 hours (E's graph window), every 10 minutes;
  time-late on a chain of 2-hour windows overlapping by 30 minutes, stitched at the overlaps'
  middles. The one adaptation: C″'s zig veto is applied at every ownship turn in the window
  (`long/windows.py` `turns_veto`), because as registered it vetoes one zig only.
- **Viewer and time-late pages show these engagements precomputed in Python.** E's in-browser
  run (spec 020/021) is not extended: at 10-minute steps a 48-hour engagement would need about
  290 graph runs on 720-row inputs (tens of megabytes of inputs, or minutes of live C in the
  browser). The pages say so.

## User Scenarios & Testing

### User Story 1 - See the systems on long engagements (Priority: P1)

The owner opens `/viewer` and `/timelate`, picks "long engagements" and a duration, and sees the
truth, each system's track and errors over the whole 6–48 hours, with ownship turns and true and
declared manoeuvres on a timeline.

**Independent Test**: build the site; both pages load the long-engagement data and draw a track,
a timeline and an error-against-time chart for each duration.

### User Story 2 - Know what it takes (Priority: P1)

The owner reads a research note (`docs/research/12-long-engagements.md`) and a blog post that
list the implementation challenges, each with a measurement where one could be made: run time
against duration and window length, error against time, where windows meet, the kernel's and the
graph's limits, data sizes, the single-zig assumptions.

**Independent Test**: every number in the note is generated by a committed script from a
committed record (`experiments/20261117-long/`).

### Edge Cases

- A window starting inside a target manoeuvre: its start moves forward to the manoeuvre's end.
- The target outruns the ownship (target up to 20 kn): range can exceed 25 km for hours; the
  plausibility limit (100 km) is not reached on these seeds; the note reports the range spread.
- A system with no answer at a row (implausible solve, IMM not started): drawn as a gap, counted
  as no answer.

## Requirements

- **FR-001**: `tma2.long.sim.generate(seed, index, hours, config, ranges)` returns arrays in the
  pack's column order and a meta the project's estimators read; deterministic for a seed.
- **FR-002**: `tma2.long.windows.window` cuts a self-contained window (time from 0, positions
  from the ownship's at its start, truth re-indexed).
- **FR-003**: `tma2.long.realtime.RealTime.run` gives E, C″ (veto at every turn), one leg (last
  30 min) and the IMM at every 10 minutes.
- **FR-004**: `tma2.long.timelate` runs `ctl` on a chain of windows and stitches one track; a
  scaling run times `ctl` on single periods of increasing length.
- **FR-005**: `tma2-long` (CLI) runs a part (`realtime`, `timelate`, `scaling`) for one
  engagement and writes an `.npz` and a `.json` with provenance; `scripts/research/long_report.py`
  writes the record's `summary.json` and `tables.md`; `scripts/research/long_viewer_data.py`
  writes the pages' data.
- **FR-006**: The viewer and time-late pages gain a long-engagement view.
- **FR-007**: Research note 12 and a kickoff and slides post.

## Success Criteria

- **SC-001**: All four durations run to the end on CI for both paths, with run times recorded.
- **SC-002**: The note states, per duration, each system's median errors over time, the share of
  rows answered, and the run time, all from the record.
- **SC-003**: Generator 0.6.0's tests pass unchanged (the kernel is not touched).
