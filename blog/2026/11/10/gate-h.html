Gate H: knowing the bearing-error model is worth almost nothing to this solve

November 10, 2026 · 9 min read

Gate H trained nothing. It asked whether realistic bearing errors leave room worth learning for: once the errors are correlated, biased, mis-scaled and include wild values, how much better is an estimator that is told the true error model than one with textbook robust weighting? On a fresh test seed the answer is: not measurably. The review panel upheld the verdict and narrowed what it may be taken to mean. A comparison it asked for then showed where the mismatch does hurt: in the segmentation, by a factor of two.

The illustrated companion is the slides post. The kickoff was here.

The question​

Every learned component in this project so far has turned out, under its control, to be a simple classical rule (research note 10). The suspected reason was that our simulated bearing errors are exactly the errors the estimator assumes: white, Gaussian, with a known σ. Where an estimator's model is the truth, there is nothing left to learn. So before any more learning work, the owner asked for a measurement: make the errors unlike the model, and see how much an estimator that knows the true error model gains over one that does not.

What was compared​

Bearing error became the sum of four parts, each of which can be switched on alone:

KindCentral severity
Correlated in timehalf the variance, 60 s correlation time
Slow bias0.5°, wandering over 30 min
Untold σtrue σ within a factor 1.5 of the stated 1°
Outliers2% of bearings, 10 σ

The severities are a declared grid the owner accepted, not measured sensor figures. The gate is decided on all four together.

Three estimators share one solve (a least-squares fit of constant-velocity legs) and are given the true manoeuvre times:

  • the plain solve, which assumes white noise;
  • a classical robust control: Huber weights against wild bearings, σ estimated from the residuals, and a correlation model fitted to the residuals. Nothing is learned;
  • an error-model-informed fit: the same solve told the true error covariance. The records call this the "attainable ceiling"; the panel found that name overstates it (below).

The statistic is R: the control's median range error over the informed fit's, minus 1. The owner fixed the threshold before any data existed: headroom is material if R's interval lies wholly above 10%.

The result​

Test seed 322, 5,203 engagements, 20,473 scored rows, all four kinds at central severity:

R = −0.4%, 97.5% interval −2.4% to +1.4%. Not material.

The plain solve is 12.9% worse than the informed fit. The robust control closes all of that gap. Both soundness checks passed on the validation seed before the test seed was generated. The forecast, written from the tuning seed, gave this verdict a probability of 0.85.

By kind, on 1,000 engagements each: outliers alone hurt the plain solve by 43% and Huber weights remove nearly all of it (R +4.2%); correlated error raises every estimator's error and leaves the most (R +3.1%); a slow bias costs little (R +1.2%).

What the panel found​

Five reviewers (statistics, estimation, domain, process, red team) each approved the verdict with findings; none raised a blocker. Three recomputed R from the per-row arrays and matched it. The process reviewer regenerated the test data from a clean clone and matched every digest. Their findings change how the result should be read.

The pooled zero is two effects cancelling. By true range:

True rangeR
under 10.7 km+4.9% [+1.8, +8.2]
10.7 to 17.2 km+3.0% [−0.2, +7.1]
17.2 to 25.3 km−2.6% [−5.7, +0.7]
over 25.3 km−5.2% [−8.3, −2.1]

At short range knowing the error model is worth about 5%. At long range the informed fit is worse than the robust control.

The reference is not a ceiling. Both the control and the informed fit sit about 25% above the Cramér–Rao bound for known manoeuvre times. That gap is larger still with no mismatch at all, so it is not error-model headroom, but it is room that a better motion model or an end-to-end estimator could claim. An earlier version of the record explained it by the solve fitting each manoeuvre with a chord. The estimation reviewer tested that and it accounts for only a part; the rest is unexplained.

The severity grid came close to fixing the outcome. For the Gaussian part of the primary condition, a linearised calculation puts the plain solve only about 3% behind exact generalised least squares. Within this error family only outliers could reach 10%, and Huber weights remove those. Stretching the grid (a 180 s correlation time, or 90% of the variance correlated) gives 8 to 12%.

The simulated mismatch is the robust control's own model family. The errors are stationary, independent of the geometry, with zero-mean independent outliers. Real bearing error is tied to ownship heading, relative bearing and the zig. The domain reviewer's candidates for hidden headroom: a bias that steps or lags at the ownship zig (range comes from the bearing-rate change across the zig), bursty or one-sided outliers, and σ that varies within an engagement.

The true manoeuvre times hide the likeliest operational cost. Correlated error and a wandering bias can look like target manoeuvres. Whether our segmenter degrades under mismatch was a registered comparison that had not been run when the verdict was computed.

On our own segmenter's output​

The comparison the panel asked for, on the validation seed (1,000 engagements), reported and not gated:

Median relative range errorNo mismatchPrimary condition
Robust control, true manoeuvre times0.0600.114
C″: our segmenter, plain solve0.1020.280
C″ + robust control0.1030.230

With the true manoeuvre times, mismatch doubles the error. On our segmenter's own output it nearly triples it: C recovers 0.92 of the gap between one leg and the oracle on clean data and 0.66 under mismatch. The robust control helps (−17.6% [−22.2, −13.7]), but it only treats the final solve, and a factor of two remains (3,460 m against 1,710 m in median range error).

So the gate's verdict and this table are both true and answer different questions. Knowing the error model is worth nothing measurable once the segmentation is right. Getting the segmentation right under mismatch is worth a factor of two. It is not yet evidence for learning: C was tuned on clean data and run unchanged, and the obvious first fix is classical.

What it means​

The statement the panel accepted:

On the true segmentation, under the declared stationary Gaussian-plus-outlier bearing-error family at the stated severities, knowing the true error model is worth no more than about 1.5% (median) to 3% (P90) in range error to the piecewise-constant-velocity least-squares solve, over a textbook robust fit. Learning the bearing-error model for this solve is not pursued.

Not shown: that this solve is near the best possible; that segmentation survives mismatch; anything about other error families or real data.

The SRD row said that with this verdict "the learning track on this estimator stops". The evidence supports the narrower sentence above. Whether the learning track as a whole stops is the owner's decision. One useful by-product stands regardless: the robust control is a cheap, classical 12% improvement over the plain solve under these errors, and costs under 2% where nothing is wrong.

What was not achieved​

  • The comparison on our own segmenter's output was promised and run late, after the verdict, on the validation seed and for the combined condition only. Which kind of mismatch breaks the segmenter, and whether it nominates falsely or misses, is not measured.
  • The cause of the solve's 25% distance from the bound.
  • Target-motion mismatch, other error families, a classical bias state, real data.
  • The IMM and EKF references are not competitive on these rows (median range error near 0.6) and leave a quarter unanswered; they were re-tuned in one pass only.

Process notes​

  • Criteria, plan and decision record were committed together before either the validation or the test seed was generated. Validation and test ran from the pull request's branch on the owner's instruction while the SRD amendment was still open; that is recorded in SRD §9.
  • Two of the gate's checks were shaped after tuning results existed and are disclosed: the form of the clean-data check (the first draft could not be decided at the sample size; the owner chose its replacement) and the efficiency band.
  • The author's first explanation of the efficiency gap was wrong and is withdrawn in the record.
  • The reviewers are agents of the same model family as the author.

Give C the robust control's σ and correlation estimates and Huber costs in its own tests, measure what is left of the factor of two, and find which kind of mismatch causes it. That is the control any learned segmenter would have to beat. If the owner does not object, it is adopted as the next spec.
