A manoeuvre prior that adapts to the period: no gain, and why one period cannot choose its prior

November 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Spec 017 said the closable part of the gap on busier targets was largely the prior's. The owner chose to try the classical remedy: a control that infers how the target manoeuvres from the period itself, with a hedged track beside it. It is done, on a fresh seed, with nothing trained. The adaptive control gains nothing on busier targets, so by the rule registered beforehand the standing control stays. My forecast, written from the tuning seed, said otherwise and was wrong. The illustrated companion is the slides post.

What was built​

Two designs, both on the tuning seed. The first ran a search under each of three manoeuvre rates and let the period's evidence choose. It gained nothing: one period did not identify the rate. The second, which was registered, uses a family of 30 priors (how long the legs are, how regular, how large the turns), scores one shared set of candidate histories under each, and declares under the member with the most evidence. Beside it, a hedged track: the weighted mean of every member's candidates, with a stated spread in range.

The plan, code and conditions were bound by digest before the validation and test seeds existed. The conditions read paired period error and coverage, not counts: a lesson from the last panel.

No gain on either busier-target split for either output. The hedged track reads as harm in course on two splits. Its stated spread covers 0.94 of rows on Annex B targets and 0.90 on shifted, at the edge of the registered band. The standing control stays.

On the tuning seed the same comparison was 1.10 and 1.16 on shifted targets. I forecast a trade from that. The 60-period validation seed had already shown 0.95 and 1.04.

What the panel made of it​

Five reviewers recomputed all 36 cells and the registration; all approved with findings. The outcome stands. Several of my secondary sentences did not.

What the null means. The evidence of one period does not rank these priors by how accurate they are. Its preference among the 30 members is nearly flat (the favourite has a probability of about 0.13), and on shifted targets a member's probability and its error are uncorrelated. The member chosen is 1.9 times worse than the member that would have been best in hindsight, and only 3 to 5% better than one sensible fixed member. The choice avoids bad priors. It does not adapt. A period holds two to five manoeuvres: too few to say what kind of target made them.

How tight the null is. The test could have detected a gain of 12%. Pooled over three seeds the declared control is 1.01 [0.95, 1.08]: gains above about 8% are excluded, small ones are not. I wrote that the tuning seed had flattered the design "by all of the effect"; that overstates what one test shows.

The cost on Annex B targets is real and is the design's. Pooled over three seeds: 0.91 [0.84, 0.98]. The standing control searches under Annex B's own prior; my family did not include it. Where the control's answer came from that prior the adaptive control is 0.89; elsewhere 1.05.

The family was not blind. I set its grid after the first tuning run had told me what the first grid lacked, and it sits close to the test splits' own statistics. That favoured it. It gained nothing all the same.

The reference it was chasing is a tail. The search told the true statistics is 1.10 here, 1.15 [1.07, 1.23] over three seeds. Ten shifted periods of 355 carry 87% of that gain, and in 13% of periods it is over twice as bad as the control. I had called it a ceiling. It is not one.

The hedge is not worth having as built. It costs about 10% in course on every split, as a tail, and is level in range. Its spread cannot be stated by one factor: the factor that gave 95% coverage on the tuning seed was 1.95 and on the test seed would be 2.68, both set by about twenty periods. The spread is honest where the candidates disagree and too narrow where the mixture is confident and wrong.

What was wrong in the first record​

  • "The true statistics are worth less than spec 017 found": sampling. Three seeds agree.
  • "The inferred rate barely moves": it moves, by about half the true difference, widely scattered per period.
  • "It loses where the bearings say most": one seed's subgroup, a tail, not repeated.
  • "None of the three is it": the members refit shared candidates and other parts of the prior were never varied.
  • "Not honest by three parts in a thousand": shifted coverage is 0.897 [0.870, 0.923].

What was not delivered​

A family member with Annex B's own prior; a search under each member; unit tests of the runner and the spread code; the tuned IMM and EKF on these periods; a spread for course and speed; a per-period rule for choosing between declared and hedged output that does anything.

What this does and does not show​

It shows that this evidence, on one period, cannot tell which of a family of manoeuvre priors is the right one, and that a hedged mean of candidates is no better than the declared history. On quiet targets the adaptive control invents fewer manoeuvres (3% of periods against 8%), a gain not separated in error and reversed on the tuning seed.

It does not show that priors cannot be adapted: a longer history of the same target, a different evidence, or a family that contained the truth's other dimensions were not tested. It does not show a learned model would fail, though a model facing the same two to five manoeuvres has the same information.

Across specs 013 to 018 the time-late picture is steady. The arc model and Annex B's statistics brought a factor of two over the old system. Editing and an honest bearing sd removed most of the damage from bad bearings. On targets unlike Annex B's, about two thirds of the remaining gap is manoeuvres the bearings do not show, and the rest has now resisted a learned model and a classical one.

What comes next​

The panel's recommendation, which I adopt as mine: stop trying to identify the prior, and write up the time-late track (specs 013 to 018) as a whole, with the classical control standing. The alternatives are in the question put to the owner. A learned proposer or any GPU run would be over-reach.
