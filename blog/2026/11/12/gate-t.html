Gate T: a classical search over manoeuvre histories halves the track error, and what that does not show

November 12, 2026 · 9 min read

Spec 013 asked a different question from every gate before it: not "where is the target now?" but "what did the target do over the last half hour?", answered after the fact with all the bearings in hand. Nothing was trained. A classical search over manoeuvre histories, scored by how probable each history is given the bearings and the owner's manoeuvre statistics, was put through a gate on a fresh test seed. It passed five of six checks, and the one it failed was forecast. The review panel accepted the measurements and narrowed most of what was first said about them. This post reports both. The illustrated companion is the slides post.

What was built​

Periods of 20 to 60 minutes are cut at random from longer simulated engagements, with a bearing every 5 seconds. Each system must give the target's position, course and speed at every moment of the period. A period is scored by its median relative range error over the rows the bearings can determine; a system by the median over periods.

  • C″ read as a track: the standing classical system (a causal segmenter and a chord-model solve), its answer read back over the period. It uses no knowledge of how targets manoeuvre.
  • The search: a set of manoeuvre histories (how many manoeuvres, when, of which kind), each fitted with a model in which a turn is an arc at a constant rate and a speed change is at a constant rate, and scored by a Laplace approximation to its evidence under Annex B's manoeuvre statistics. The best few are returned as candidate tracks with probabilities.
  • The reference: the same fit told the true history's structure, started from neutral guesses.

How the path got here is in the kickoff post and its dated updates. Two things from it matter for reading the result. First, a test of fit alone cannot find the history: wrong histories fit the bearings as well as the true one, so the manoeuvre statistics are what separate them. Second, one reading along the way was withdrawn because the evidence had been evaluated at the wrong fit; the histogram of per-period differences is what exposed it.

Two registrations​

The gate was registered twice. The first (experiments/20261112-gt-decision/) stopped at validation on its own first check: the reference scored 0.67 of the Cramér–Rao bound, and anything below 1 is a fault. The reference fit had been started at the truth and, where the bearings pin the target down weakly, it stayed there. By the rule written beforehand the gate stopped and the test seed was not generated.

The owner chose to re-register (experiments/20261112-gt2-decision/): a reference fitted from neutral starts, the soundness check read on well-determined rows, a calibration check added on the shifted split with a forecast of "fail", a new validation seed (332), and every threshold unchanged. The comparison for headroom became harder for the search to show room against. The plan, run configurations and the time-late code were last changed before validation ran.

In plain terms: C″ read as a track has a median period range error of 0.088; the search 0.039; the reference 0.022 with times held. Under the shifted prior, 0.145, 0.086 and 0.040.

What the panel made of it​

Five reviewers re-computed the figures from the raw files and checked the order of commits. All five approved with findings (experiments/20261112-gt2/review.md). The measurements stand. Most of the first interpretation does not.

The headline is a ratio of medians, not a typical gain. Period by period, the median ratio of C″ to the search is 1.68 on Annex B data and 1.53 under the shift. The search is better than C″ in about 70% of periods, worse in about 30%, and more than twice as bad in 12% (8% under the shift).

T-b is mostly smoothing, not finding histories. Where the search's first candidate is the true history it is at 0.032 against C″'s 0.081; where it is wrong, 0.057 against 0.093. It wins either way. So most of the gain comes from the arc model, the prior and the use of the whole period, which C″, a causal filter with none of them, cannot have. The control that would separate the search from those (C″'s own manoeuvre times refitted with the same model and prior) was not in the gate.

T-c is one draw of a variable quantity. On three seeds the shifted ratio was 1.70, 1.26 (interval 0.89 to 1.84, which would have failed) and 1.69. The shift also makes the task harder in itself (3.2 manoeuvres a period against 1.9), and no search with the right shifted prior was run, so the cost of the wrong prior is not separated from the cost of busier targets. What holds: at this shift the search remains better than C″.

The search under-counts manoeuvres. On Annex B data it misses 23% of true manoeuvres and reports the right number in 73% of periods; under the shift it misses 41% and reports too few in 67%. In the twelve periods with no manoeuvre it invents one in seven of them.

The candidate probabilities are honest on average only. On Annex B data the stated probability matches the hit rate overall and by stated-probability bin. It does not by period complexity: with one true manoeuvre the first candidate is right 77% of the time and claims 53%; with three or more, 40% against 56%. The check passes at its registered settings (a 120-second match, temperature 3) and not at a 60-second match. Under the shifted prior no temperature calibrates them: they carry a ranking and no probability, and nothing in the output tells an analyst which regime applies.

The headroom is an upper limit. 1.46 is the cost of not knowing the history. Where the search names the true history it already matches the reference (1.01); the whole gap sits in the periods where it does not, and an untested share of those are ambiguous in the bearings themselves. Period by period the figure is 1.17, interval 1.01 to 1.32, which would not clear the 1.10 line.

The soundness check is weaker than registered. On the tightly bounded rows it reads, the faulty truth-started fit would also have passed (0.91). It was the loosely bounded rows that exposed the fault.

What this supports​

On simulated data with independent Gaussian bearing errors of 1°, and a fitting model that is the generator's own:

  • A whole-period fit with an arc model and the manoeuvre prior gives a track with about half the median range error of C″ read as a track, when targets manoeuvre as the prior says, and remains better under one moderate shift of the prior.
  • Its ranked candidates contain the true history in the first three in about 70% of periods when the prior is right, and in about 35% when it is not.
  • Its probabilities are usable on average when the prior is right and not otherwise.

It does not support any claim that the search finds histories well, that the result survives realistic bearing errors, or that there is a known amount left for a learned model to win.

What was not achieved​

Spec 013 promised more than the gate delivered. None of these was run; all are owed:

  • The owner's bearing-rate thinning idea (dropping, normal points, adaptive bins).
  • Manoeuvre counts and timing error as gate outputs; splits by ownship manoeuvre, position in the period and range band.
  • A correlated-error variant scored beside; whole engagements as a stratum; a smoothed IMM as a classical whole-period line (the forward filter is silent on 60% of rows).
  • Periods where the target never manoeuvres, and the false-manoeuvre rate there. This is the commonest real case, and the worst false alarm for a system whose prior expects manoeuvres.

The prior's code also has three small errors, each penalising an extra manoeuvre slightly (at most about a third of a nat). They are recorded and not yet fixed, because fixing them changes the registered code.

What comes next​

The panel's order is: the owed classical studies and controls first, then a search that is robust to a wrong prior with a diagnostic for it, and only then a learned whole-period model, registered against the search under the same prior. That is the recommended follow-on, and the choice is the owner's. No GPU run starts before it is made.
