Long engagements: the carried prior against longer windows

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Spec 029 found that on held-course 1-hour windows, carrying the previous window's state as a prior (ctl-prior) beats solving the window alone. Its panel called that "more data beats no data" and asked for the fair comparisons: ctl on its standing 2-hour windows, and a joint solve over the turn-free hour and the hour before it.

The result: the registered rule reads "ctl-prior worse" against every comparator. The clean comparison is the joint solve. It uses the same past as the carried prior and no later bearings, and its error is 0.774 [0.632, 0.954] of the prior's. A 1-hour window with a carried prior is not a substitute for using the bearings themselves.

Spec: specs/031-held-comparators/. Record: experiments/20261120-cmp/.

The design​

  • Engagements: spec 029's held-course engagements: ownship legs of 1–2 h, the tended contact, 60 engagements of 6 and 12 h.
  • Systems, each on its own windows and read on the same turn-free 1-hour windows:
    • (a) 1-hour ctl-prior, spec 029's system;
    • (b) ctl and ctl-prior on 2-hour windows overlapping 30 min, stitched mid-overlap;
    • (c) ctl on the union of the turn-free hour and the hour before it: the joint solve.
  • Estimands, each comparator's error ÷ (a)'s in range, as a geometric mean with a bootstrap over engagements:
    • D1: 2-hour ctl;
    • D2: the joint solve;
    • D3: 2-hour ctl-prior, added on tuning before validation.
  • Rule: "ctl-prior better" or "worse" when the interval excludes 1. The rule was registered before seeds 365 and 366 existed (experiments/20261120-cmp-decision/).

What happened​

Test: seed 366. 179 decided turn-free windows from 56 engagements, scored once. Soundness passed.

  • Spec 029's gain replicates at 1.71 [1.40, 2.14]. Quote it as about 1.7 to 2.4.

  • The forecasts were 1.1–1.3 for D1 and D2, and wrong in direction. Tuning had pointed that way on about 34 windows, but it compared (a) from spec 029's run on another CPU family with (b) and (c) from this run, which spec 024's gotcha forbids. D3's forecast was right in direction, and the effect was larger than forecast.

What the panel narrowed​

Three reviewers each approved with findings (experiments/20261120-cmp/review.md). Every decided number was reproduced independently.

  • D2 is the clean comparison.

    • D1 and D3 take 98% of their rows from a 2-hour window that contains an ownship turn, and that window runs a median of 29 min past the hour.
    • So they also measure geometry and latency. The joint solve has neither advantage and still wins.

  • These are averages with wide spread. The 1-hour prior is better in about 4 windows in 10 and twice as bad in about a third.

  • The readings are conservative. Four windows where 1-hour ctl-prior gave no answer at all are excluded, and the comparators answered all four.

  • Heterogeneity. D2 is 0.62 on 6-hour engagements and 0.90 [0.69, 1.17] on 12-hour ones.

  • 2-hour ctl-prior is not shown best here.

    • Its edge over 2-hour ctl (1.49 held, median 1.14; 1.26 one-turn, median 1.01) is supplement only, and validation went the other way.
    • The registered support for it is spec 028's test.

  • Cost and latency. Per engagement-hour, the 2-hour job costs about 2.1× the 1-hour one, but both run several systems, so no single system's cost is known. A 2-hour estimate waits for up to an hour of later bearings and updates every 90 min instead of 45.

  • Limits:

    • ownship legs of 1–2 h;
    • contact 1;
    • one simulator;
    • windows placed past true target manoeuvres;
    • spec 028's hand-over defect kept.

    Legs longer than the standing 2-hour window, the owner's blind case at the standing configuration, were not tested.

What it means for the owner's question​

The previous track does constrain a blind leg: spec 029 shows that. But the same past bearings used directly in a joint fit constrain it better than a Gaussian summary of them. The carried prior compresses the past into four numbers and a covariance. Where those bearings are available, fitting them beats summarising them.

Spec 029's recommendation, ctl-prior on 1-hour windows, is withdrawn. What stands is spec 028's: ctl-prior with its guard on 2-hour windows for tended contacts up to 12 h.

Next (the owner's choice)​

  • Recommended default: close the long-engagement track as a simulation study, with ctl on 2-hour windows and spec 028's ctl-prior standing. Write one synthesis of specs 023 to 030.
  • Alternatives:
    • Legs longer than the window: ownship legs of 2–4 h with 2-hour windows, compared on past bearings only, reporting the 2-hour hand-overs' guard rate and coverage.
    • Reopen with new information: measured realism, an ownship steered by its own track, or association.
