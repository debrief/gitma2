Cramér–Rao bound and efficiency

Source: src/tma2/references/crb.py; used by tma2-crb and tma2-eval. Requirements: SRD §5 (efficiency), constitution IV.

Fisher information​

For the constant-velocity parameters θ = (x₀, y₀, vₓ, v_y) anchored at a reference time and independent Gaussian bearing noise σ, the Fisher information from the valid bearings up to row k, evaluated on the true trajectory, is

I = Σ_i g_i g_iᵀ / σ², g_i = (dy_i/ρ_i², −dx_i/ρ_i², τ_i dy_i/ρ_i², −τ_i dx_i/ρ_i²),

with (dx, dy) the true target offset from ownship, ρ its length and τ_i the time since the anchor. Any unbiased estimator's covariance is at least I⁻¹.

Bounds on the reported quantities​

By the delta method, var(f(θ)) ≥ ∇fᵀ I⁻¹ ∇f for range r = ‖p(t_k) − o_k‖, course c = atan2(vₓ, v_y) and speed s = ‖v‖. gitma2 reports the standard deviations in the metric units: relative range (σ_r / r), course in degrees, speed in knots. Before the zig (one leg) and in degenerate geometry I is singular (condition number above 10¹²): the bound is undefined and the row is counted in crb_singular_rate, never as 0 or ∞. Singularity is judged on the Jacobi-scaled matrix, so position in metres and velocity in m/s do not inflate the condition number.

Three models (tma2-crb --model)​

  • Full window (full_window, the default): constant velocity over all bearings up to k: the right bound when the target holds course throughout (Gate 1's data).
  • Known manoeuvres (known_manoeuvres): the generator's own manoeuvre kinematics (an arc at constant speed for a course change, constant acceleration for a speed change), with each manoeuvre's start time, duration and kind known and its size unknown, and all bearings up to k. The bound for an estimator that knows when and how the target manoeuvred (the oracle's regime): position continuity carries the earlier legs across the manoeuvre. It is defined inside manoeuvres too; the Jacobian of the positions is taken by central differences, and the model reproduces the stored truth to under 1 cm (float32 storage). Letting the positions inside a manoeuvre be free instead would decouple the legs, which is no better than the per-leg bound.
  • Per leg (per_leg, a diagnostic): constant velocity over the current leg's bearings only. It ignores the earlier legs, so an estimator that knows the manoeuvre times can legitimately beat it; on manoeuvring data it is singular on most post-manoeuvre rows (no ownship manoeuvre on the new leg). Panel round 1 of P0 found the earlier text calling it the oracle's bound wrong.

Efficiency​

For a slice, efficiency_<metric> = median over rows with a regular bound of |e_i| / σ_i, divided by 0.6745 (the median of a standard normal's absolute value). Each error is normalised by its own row's bound before the median, so slices that mix σ = 1° and 2° rows, or near and far rows, are not biased; dividing a median error by a median bound would be (it drifts from 0.67 to below 0.6 as the bounds spread). An unbiased estimator at the bound with Gaussian errors scores 1; batch least squares scores close to 1 on held-course rows at σ = 1° (P0 checks speed).

Caveats:

  • The bound holds for unbiased estimators. Estimators with priors (the physics layer's ridge and speed-continuity priors) or with plausibility clipping can legitimately score below 1.
  • Range and course bounds come from the delta method, which is poor when the bound is large relative to the value (σ_r / r or σ_c large: young legs, weak geometry); read efficiency on those slices with care. Course errors wrap at 180°.
  • On manoeuvring data the full-window bound assumes a model the target does not follow; use the known-manoeuvres bound there.

A learned estimator whose efficiency is flat across range and bearing-rate bands while classical's tracks the geometry has hit an approximation floor rather than an information floor: this is the diagnostic gitma's record lacked (docs/research/01).
