## What is measured

- **E1**: on contact 1, the contact the ownship tends, the per-window ratio ctl ÷ ctl-carry in
  median range error. The estimate is its geometric mean. **ctl-carry is non-inferior if the
  95% interval's lower limit is at least 0.95.** If so, carrying is adopted for long chains as
  a cost measure. The CPU hours of the two searches are reported beside, and so is the same
  ratio on contact 2.
- **E2**: ctl's median error on the untended contact 2 ÷ on contact 1, in range bands of 2–10,
  10–20 and 20–40 km. The estimate is the geometric mean over the bands. A magnitude, with no
  rule.
- **E3**: the IMM's median error in hours 6–12 ÷ hours 0–6 on the 12-hour engagements. **Drift
  is shown if the interval lies wholly above 1.**

All intervals come from a bootstrap over engagements (2,000 resamples, seed 25).

## Soundness (validation, seed 350; a failure stops the study)

- S1: at least 95% of contact 1's windows ran.
- S2: ctl ÷ the true-history fit lies in [0.8, 4].
- S3: contact 1's share of bearings held lies in [0.6, 0.95].

## Can each rule come out either way?

- **E1**: at 60 engagements the interval's half-width is about 0.05. The rule passes if the
  estimate is above about 1.00 and fails below it.
- **E3**: at 30 twelve-hour engagements the half-width is about 0.18. Drift is shown if the
  estimate is above about 1.2.

## Forecasts (restating tuning; not independent predictions)

- **Soundness**: passes.
- **E1**: non-inferior, about three times in four. The per-window median is 1.00, and the
  tuning mean of 1.05 rests on a few windows that were twice better.
- **E2**: about 1.2 to 1.4, with an interval that includes 1. Matched by range, most of spec
  024's three-to-fivefold cost is geometry.
- **E3**: no drift shown.

Listed in `decision.json`. The two that matter most:
