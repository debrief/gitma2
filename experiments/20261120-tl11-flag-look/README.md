# Spec 030 follow-on: is a per-period consistency flag worth building? (development look)

Owner 2026-11-20: option 1 of spec 030's panel order, "a consistency flag on the carried prior's
distance, tested on whether it finds the twice-as-bad periods". Before building or registering
anything, this look reads the records spec 030 already committed (refinement seeds 360 and 361,
validation 362, test 363; all spent). No new run, no new seed, nothing trained. Readings:
`readings.txt`, generated by `scripts/research/tl11_flag_look.py` (regenerates byte for byte).

"Twice as bad": a later period where the carried prior's period median range error is over twice
`ctl` alone's (spec 025's rows). On Annex B with k4 that is 8 to 12 periods a seed (7 to 11%).

## What it shows

1. **The evidence is a weak flag.** The mixture variants recorded each period's log Bayes factor
   of the carried prior against the standing prior. As a detector of twice-as-bad periods its AUC
   is 0.52 to 0.78 (eight seed × split × variant cells). Flagging at a factor below 1 and falling
   back to `ctl` alone:
   - catches 13 to 54% of the bad periods;
   - loses 5 to 32% of the twice-as-good ones;
   - moves alone ÷ system from 0.98 to 1.03, 1.62 to 1.79, 1.53 to 1.63 and 1.57 to 1.69 for the
     uninflated prior, and by −0.02 to +0.24 for inflation 2.

   This is what the development mixtures (`m50`, `k2m50`) already were; spec 030's choice rule
   excluded them on coverage.
2. **The ceiling is low.** A consistency flag compares the carried prior with what the period's
   own bearings say. Its best possible input is the truth's own distance from the carried prior,
   an oracle. As a detector of the same periods that oracle reaches:
   - an AUC of 0.64 to 0.69 for the uninflated prior;
   - an AUC of 0.45 to 0.76 for k4, above 0.60 only on test 363's Annex B.

   Most twice-as-bad periods are not periods where the carried state is wrong.
3. **What the twice-as-bad periods are.** In them `ctl` alone does unusually well, with a median
   range error of 0.02 to 0.12 against 0.21 to 0.42 elsewhere. k4 does somewhat worse, 0.09 to 0.60
   against 0.12 to 0.20. The joint solve is usually also worse than alone there. About half have an
   ownship turn of their own (0.22 to 0.73), no different from all periods. So "twice as bad" is
   mostly a period whose own fit landed close, which the carried prior then pulled away; it is
   not mostly a carried state that was wrong.

## Recommendation (owner's choice)

Do not register a consistency flag on the carried prior's distance: even an oracle version would
find at most about two in three of the periods it is meant to find on k4, and usually fewer. The
evidence flag is the realisable version and gains 0 to 10%, within the seed-to-seed spread
(Annex B alone ÷ k4 was 1.22 to 1.59 across seeds). A flag that asks whether the period's own fit
is already determined (its own bound or covariance tight) targets what the bad periods actually
are, but that is the joint/alone choice the carried prior was meant to replace, and it would be
scored against the joint solve, which is standing.

## Limits

- Development reading on spent seeds; the flags were not tuned and nothing here is a registered
  result. Thresholds other than a factor of 1 were not read.
- The mixtures' chains diverge from the plain chains after a period they declared differently, so
  pairing a mixture's log odds with the plain variant's errors is exact for second periods and
  approximate for third ones (few: 5 to 14 a split).
- "Twice as bad" is a ratio of two noisy medians; with 7 to 11% of periods it rests on 8 to 24
  periods a cell.
