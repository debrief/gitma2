Several periods of one target across long gaps: what joining depends on

November 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Spec 025 found that one solve through the gaps beats solving each period of a target alone, across gaps of 5 to 30 minutes. This development study stretched the gaps to 30–90 minutes. It ran twice on one spent seed: once with an ownship that turns often, and once with one that holds course. Nothing was trained and nothing here is a registered result.

What the data support, as narrowed by the review panel:

  • Joining helps a period that has no ownship turn of its own, by about 2, across gaps under about 70 minutes.
  • Beyond 70 minutes the readings fall below 1 in both runs, but they are not separated.
  • The true gap structure would still help there. What falls short is choosing the gap history: in most engagements the evidence does not prefer the true one.

The illustrated companion is the slides post.

What was run​

  • Engagements: 40 six-hour engagements from the long simulator (specs 023–026), with Annex B targets and no realism layer.
  • Layout: each engagement is cut into 2 to 4 periods of 15–30 minutes, with gaps of 30–90 minutes.
  • Two runs on the same engagements, targets and layouts:
    • the ownship turns every 20–40 minutes;
    • the ownship holds each leg 1–2 hours.
  • Systems, unchanged from spec 030:
    • ctl alone on each period;
    • the joint solve over the span, with the gaps' bearings masked;
    • the carried prior k4.
  • Seed: spent seed 348.
  • Diagnostic: on the 21 engagements with a gap of at least 70 minutes, the evidence of the declared gap history was compared with the true history fitted the same way.

  • Whether a period has its own ownship turn matters most. Without one, joining helps by about 2, separated in both runs below 70 minutes. With one, it reads 1.1–1.25, not separated. On rows a period determines alone, alone ÷ joint is 0.89 in both runs.
  • Gap length.
    • Of 18 band intervals one is separated: holding course, 50–70 minutes.
    • A trend with gap length is not separated in either run.
    • In the turning run the median period at 50–70 minutes favours ctl alone (0.80).
    • The bands were chosen after looking at the data.
  • Beyond 70 minutes the geometric means read below 1 in both runs (0.80, 0.71), with medians 0.95 and 0.88. Neither is separated. The two runs barely agree period by period (r = 0.15), so they are not a replication.
  • Holding course roughly doubles everyone's error (by geometric mean; the median for alone rises by 1.49).
  • k4, as tuned on spec 030's short gaps and propagated at constant velocity, is level with ctl alone. After an hour its uncertainty covers almost everything.
  • Cost. The joint solve takes 11 to 16 times as long as ctl alone.

Beyond about an hour: the gap history is not chosen well​

  • The information exists given the history. The span fitted with the true gap structure still beats ctl alone beyond 70 minutes: 2.35 [0.96, 5.98] holding course, 2.48 [1.30, 4.90] turning. The joint solve is about 3 times that reference's error.
  • The evidence rarely separates the true gap history from a simpler one. The joint solve declares fewer manoeuvres than happened: 123 against 178.
    • In most engagements the Laplace evidence does not prefer the true history.
    • Most differences are within ±10 units: the declared history is better by more than 10 in 5 engagements, the truth in 3.
    • Search shortfall is a minority.
  • Whether a better search would help is not resolved. The diagnostic's re-run ran on another CPU family. In 5 of 21 engagements it declared a different history from the scored run, so its cross-tabulation against errors is not a result.
  • A limit of the comparison. The "true" history is a prior-fitted Laplace mode, and pairing defects of 1–3.5 nats are known.

What it supports, and what not​

  • A suggestion, not a rule: join a period that has no ownship turn of its own with its neighbours across gaps up to about an hour. Beyond that, nothing here shows that joining helps or harms. This is one spent seed, with bands chosen after the data.
  • Not to be claimed:
    • that joining fails beyond an hour;
    • that the limit is identifiability rather than search;
    • that carried priors carry nothing in general.
  • Limits:
    • the long simulator's ownship steers on the true target;
    • C″'s start reads only the first ownship turn;
    • 20 to 30 periods per band.

Follow-up, 2026-11-21: the evidence re-check​

The owner chose to re-check the evidence before deciding (experiments/20261121-tl13-evidence/). This time each engagement's histories and errors came from one job on one machine. Three statements above change:

  • A gain from the true gap history is not shown, at this size. Refitted the same way, the declared history with its gap manoeuvres replaced by the true ones is level with it after long gaps: 0.96 [0.67, 1.40]. Many of those refits sit in poor minima, so this may be a fitting failure rather than an absence of information. The 2.35 and 2.48 above come from the whole true history, which also knows the observed periods' manoeuvres. "What falls short is choosing the gap history" is withdrawn.
  • Beyond about 70 minutes nothing is shown either way (alone ÷ joint 0.97 [0.58, 1.73], median 1.03). The interval still contains the earlier 0.71; the shift is the search's known sensitivity to the CPU family, so read neither figure as a direction.
  • The truth's refit does not beat the declared history. Against the better declared fit, the truth's evidence is higher by more than 3 units in 1 of 21 engagements, lower in 11, and within 3 in 9. The truth is refitted from neutral starts and such refits can lose 10 to 24 units, so this does not show that the search is not the limit.

The suggestion is now narrower. Joining helps a period without its own ownship turn across gaps under about an hour. Beyond that, nothing is shown either way.

Owner 2026-11-21: the gap track is closed. Where several periods of one target are separated by gaps under about an hour, the joint solve stays the standing time-late system. Nothing beyond that is claimed, and no further study is planned unless the owner reopens the track.
