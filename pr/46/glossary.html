Glossary

TMA and ML terms as used in gitma2. Terms carried from gitma keep gitma's meaning.

  • Autopilot oscillation — generator 0.6.0's second kind of ownship wander: a sinusoid of 1–3° amplitude and 2–6 min period on the course of every steady ownship leg.
  • Batch least squares — the classical reference: a Levenberg–Marquardt fit of a constant-velocity target to all bearings so far, from a coarse range–course–speed grid.
  • Bearing — true direction from ownship to the target, clockwise from north, radians internally.
  • Changepoint weight — the physics layer's input c ∈ [0, 1] per row: how free the target's velocity is to change there. The M largest become candidate knots; 0 everywhere is one leg.
  • Confirmation — C's decision step: a nominated transition is accepted only if the physics-layer solve on its own prefix, with knots at both its ends, lowers the cost by more than τ = 13.8 (χ² with 4 degrees of freedom); see segmentation.
  • Contamination — the share of a reported steady leg's rows that the truth labels as transition; Gate S's primary segmentation measure (median and P90 over prefixes).
  • Control (model) — a variant trained only to test the pipeline, never carried: direct regression head, shuffled labels (must score no better than the design without learning: naive for a regressor, batch least squares for a physics-layer model), time-late (must score at least as well as the causal model).
  • Coverage (interval) — the share of rows whose error lies inside the stated interval (z·sd); a calibrated 95% interval covers 95% of rows. Spec 008; see calibration. Not to be confused with Coverage (segmentation).
  • Coverage — the share of truth-steady rows that lie inside reported steady legs.
  • Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) — the lowest variance any unbiased estimator can reach given the bearings' Fisher information; batch least squares sits at it on a straight leg. Three models: full window, known manoeuvres, per leg (see CRB).
  • Classical control (C′, C″) — Gate 2's rules that stand in for what E learned (panel round 1): C′ raises C's confirmation threshold by τ/2 for target transitions at the ownship zig ± 6 rows (the zig veto); C″ adds a fixed fading memory, clip(0.73 − 0.0079 × age, ±2) on C's log-weights. Nothing in them is learned; see Gate 2 model.
  • CUSUM — cumulative-sum change detector: S(k) = max(0, S(k − 1) + ν(k)² − 1 − δ) on normalised innovations, an alarm while S exceeds a tuned threshold; a Gate D control (see detector).
  • Decision table — E-s's training data (spec 007): for each of C's confirmation decisions within τ/2 of its threshold, the bound-normalised error at every later scored row with the transition and without it, C's other decisions held; see Gate 2 model.
  • Detectability — a change's oracle cost drop: the physics layer's cost on the prefix with every true knot, against the same without this change's (χ² with 4 degrees of freedom); note 08's bands are below 13.3, 13.3–50, 50–200 and at least 200.
  • Efficiency — the median over a slice's rows of error ÷ that row's CRB, divided by 0.6745, so an unbiased estimator at the bound scores 1; a flat efficiency across geometry slices is the signature of an approximation floor.
  • Circular log score — the log density of a stated course distribution at the true course (on the circle); a proper score, higher is better. Gate 4's G4-c compares it with the uniform's (−log 2π), the score of saying "course unknown".
  • Interval score — Gneiting and Raftery's score for a central (1 − α) interval: its width plus 2/α times the distance by which the truth falls outside it; lower is better, and it rewards calibration and sharpness together. Gate 3's score of record (G3-c).
  • Fisher information — the curvature of the bearings' log-likelihood in the target state; its inverse is the Cramér–Rao bound. See CRB.
  • G5 rows — σ = 1° scenarios scored at zig + 5, 10, 15 and 20 min where the window reaches them (gitma Annex C); one scenario contributes several rows, so bootstraps resample scenarios.
  • Fading memory — a fixed recency profile on the bearings' log-weights (newer bearings weigh more); the classical stand-in for what E-w learned. See Classical control.
  • Gap recovered — (median one-leg error − median segmentation error) ÷ (median one-leg error − median oracle error) on σ = 1° G5 rows: one leg scores 0, the oracle 1; S2 and S4 compare it between segmenters.
  • Gate — a pre-registered go/no-go check (P0, Gate 1, Gate D, Gate 2), judged by the agent review panel on a fresh test seed.
  • Gate D — the steady-leg detector's gate (SRD Annex A §A.5): detection delay and misses against CUSUM and GLR at matched false-alarm rates, held-leg false alarms, and the physics layer's error fed by the detector's knots against the IMM and batch least squares.
  • Gate S — the steady-leg segmentation gate (SRD Annex A §A.9): S1 O's ownship contamination, S2 C against GLR + confirm, S3 L's contamination and false splits, S4 L against the reference (the only learning claim).
  • Generator version — the algorithm that turns (seed, index, ranges) into a scenario; gitma2 implements gitma's 0.5.0 and reproduces its files byte for byte. Generator 0.6.0 adds the MAN2 model behind new ranges fields, so 0.5.0 ranges still give 0.5.0's bytes.
  • GLR — generalised likelihood ratio test for a velocity change inside a sliding window, the window's one-leg fit projected out; a Gate D control.
  • Grid start — gitma's coarse initialisation for batch least squares: the lowest-cost candidate of 12 ranges × 8 courses × 3 speeds along the first valid bearing; the Gate 1 model corrects it before the solve.
  • Helm wander — generator 0.6.0's first kind of ownship wander: a first-order autoregressive course error (σ 1.5°, τ 60 s) clipped at ± 4°, on steady ownship rows only.
  • Held leg — rows where the target's constant-velocity model holds: before any manoeuvre, or more than five minutes after the last one ended.
  • Held-out split — scenarios drawn from parameter ranges outside training (longer initial ranges), to measure extrapolation.
  • IMM — interacting multiple-model tracker (constant velocity plus coordinated turn), tuned on validation and frozen; the reference for manoeuvring targets.
  • Implicit gradient — the derivative of a converged solve's solution with respect to its inputs, from the optimality condition instead of unrolling the iterations; the Gate 1 model takes one exact-Hessian Newton step from the detached optimum to get it (Gate 1 model).
  • Instantaneous / time-late — the causal estimate at each timestep, using bearings up to now, versus the estimate using the whole window.
  • MAN2 — the problem generator 0.6.0 simulates (SRD Annex B): target legs of N(25, 9.1) min with a 5-min floor (5% at or under 10 min), ended by a course (80%), speed (10%) or combined (10%) change of about a minute or more, in 40–120 min windows (up to 720 rows), with ownship helm wander or autopilot oscillation.
  • Manoeuvre span — the sample interval of one target course or speed change, from the first sample after it starts to the first sample after it ends.
  • Reliability — for a stated probability P that an event happens (Gate 4: the course lies within ±X), the hit rate minus the mean P; 0 when P is honest. Checked overall and in equal-count bins of P (a reliability diagram).
  • NEES — normalised estimation error squared, eᵀP⁻¹e for the 4-state error e and stated covariance P; χ² with 4 degrees of freedom when P is right (median 3.357, 95% point 9.488). Gate 3's G3-b uses the share of rows within the χ²₄ 95% point (owner, 2026-11-08); the median over the χ²₄ median is reported, not gated.
  • Nomination — a candidate transition put to confirmation: a raised GLR alarm (re-tested while raised) or a transition of the additive leg search.
  • Oracle ceiling — the physics layer fed with the true manoeuvre times and kinds (knots at each manoeuvre's start and end, the chord of a turn, ridge τ_r = 3 m/s): the reference for any "segment, then solve" design. It is not a strict upper bound: on noise-free data most of its error after a turn is the chord approximation; with noise, giving the in-turn bearings zero weight does not help, and at σ = 1° it is 1.2–1.3 × the known-manoeuvres Cramér–Rao bound, the information limit.
  • Ownship — the observing platform, whose track is known exactly.
  • Physics layer — see the algorithm note; the declared, differentiable piecewise-constant-velocity batch solve that produces every gitma2 estimate; with trivial inputs it equals batch least squares.
  • Relaxed acceptance — E-s's differentiable stand-in for C's accept rule: p = σ((Δ − τ′ − s)/T), with s the evidence's capped threshold shift and T a declared temperature; the loss weights each decision's two errors by p and 1 − p.
  • Ridge prior — the physics layer's prior that a new leg's velocity stays near the old one (√c · Δv / 3 m/s per knot), which keeps a leg a few samples old finite.
  • Scenario — one ownship-versus-target run: metadata, two truth tracks and a bearing series.
  • Seed — a 64-bit integer that determines a dataset; per-scenario seeds derive from it, so the same seed gives the same scenario in gitma and gitma2.
  • Semi-Markov likelihood — L's training loss: the negative log-likelihood of the truth segmentation over every segmentation of the search's lattice (legs and transitions with their own lengths), normalised by a log-sum-exp forward recursion.
  • Settled row — a scored row on a held leg (gitma Annex C §C.5's definition).
  • Sharpness — how narrow an estimator's stated uncertainty is (median stated sd), reported beside coverage: a calibrated estimator should be as sharp as it can while still covering.
  • Speed-continuity prior — the physics layer's prior that speed carries through a course change (‖v_after‖ − ‖v_before‖)/1 m/s, skipping the chord leg inside the turn.
  • Steady-leg detector — the first learned component (SRD Annex A): per-timestep manoeuvre probability, changepoint weights and manoeuvre kind, feeding the physics layer.
  • Transition — the interval of a target change between two steady legs, from its first changed row to the row where the new course and speed hold (a manoeuvre span).
  • Zig veto — raising C's threshold for a target transition at the ownship's own manoeuvre, where a target knot would absorb the bearing-rate change and make range unobservable; what E-s mostly learned. See Classical control.
  • Zig — ownship's deliberate course change that makes range observable; gate times count from the first sample after the first turn completes.
  • Zig leg — the steady target leg that contains the ownship zig. On MAN2 data the target can change before the zig, so the noise-free and efficiency checks fit this leg alone (spec 006 research R3, the restatement of gitma's "before the manoeuvre").
