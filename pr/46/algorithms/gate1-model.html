The Gate 1 model

Sources:

  • src/tma2/models/ (rows, model, loss, train, predict, export);
  • the physics layer it drives: physics layer.

Requirements: SRD §4 and §5 (Gate 1), constitution principles III and VI, and spec 003.

The model is a small causal transformer that reads the bearing history and supplies three inputs that physics cannot know. The physics layer produces the estimate, with no knots: Gate 1's data is constant velocity. The last layer of every head starts at zero, so the untrained model is batch least squares from the classical grid start (tested).

Inputs and features​

One window of R = 240 rows (40 min at 10 s) gives these inputs:

  • bearings z, with valid flags v;
  • ownship positions (oₓ, o_y) and times t;
  • the noise σ;
  • the row k whose state is wanted.

Samples after k are ignored.

Each row has seven features. Every scale is fixed: no statistic is taken from the data.

  • sin z · v and cos z · v;
  • v;
  • (oₓ − oₓ,₀) / 10 km and (o_y − o_y,₀) / 10 km;
  • t / 1 h;
  • σ / 2°.

Encoder​

A linear embedding to width W = 64 is followed by two pre-norm blocks and a final layer norm. Each block is written out in standard operators, so an independent port can reproduce it. With h the [R, W] activations, LN the layer norm and 4 heads of size d = 16:

  • Attention. Q, K, V = LN(h)·W_q, LN(h)·W_k, LN(h)·W_v, then h ← h + W_o · concat_heads softmax(QKᵀ/√d + M)·V. M is −∞ where key j > query i (causal), and 0 elsewhere.
  • Feed-forward. h ← h + W₂ · relu(W₁ · LN(h)), with an inner width of 128.

The time-late control (d) uses no causal mask. It masks padded rows as keys instead.

Heads​

Each head is Linear(W, W) → ReLU → Linear(·), and its last layer is zero-initialised.

  • Per-bearing log-weight, from row i's activation: ℓ_i = 2 tanh(·), so each bearing's weight exp(ℓ_i) lies in [e⁻², e²].
  • Initial-state correction, from row k's activation: (Δ log range, Δ course, Δ log speed), applied to the grid start's state at row k.
    • Range from ownship is multiplied by exp(Δ log range).
    • The velocity is rotated clockwise by Δ course and scaled by exp(Δ log speed).
    • The corrected state at k is mapped back to the window-start state θ₀ = (p_k − u t_k, u), which starts the solve.
  • Covariance log-scale, from row k's activation: s = 2 tanh(·). The reported covariance is the layer's covariance × e^(2s).

The grid start is gitma's coarse grid along the first valid bearing up to k:

  • 12 log-spaced ranges from 1 to 40 km;
  • 8 courses;
  • speeds of 2, 5 and 9 m/s;
  • the candidate with the lowest cost is chosen, the first one on ties.

The exported graph computes it in tensor operations (grid_start_tensor). It equals the numba version to 1e-14.

Physics​

The physics layer runs with M = 0 knots and the log-weights ℓ, from θ₀, for K = 100 damped Gauss–Newton iterations in double precision. The output is the state (x, y, vₓ, v_y) at row k and its covariance σ²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ mapped to row k, plus two flags:

  • cov_ok: the covariance is valid;
  • plausible: the solution is plausible.

Loss​

The loss is computed on scored rows whose covariance is valid. The errors of (log range, vₓ, v_y) at row k are divided by the row's full-window Cramér–Rao standard deviations, computed from the truth, giving e. The loss is

L = mean(min(‖e‖², 100)) + 1.0 · mean(min(Σ β-NLL, 100)) + 0.1 · mean(min(‖e_init‖², 100))

where:

  • β-NLL = stopgrad(v^β) · (½ log v + e²/2v), with β = 0.5;
  • v is the calibrated variance ÷ the bound's variance, per component;
  • e_init is the same normalised error of the corrected initial state.

The initial-state head gets no gradient through the converged solve (below), so the auxiliary term trains it.

Gradient through the solve​

Training. The forward pass runs the K iterations without a tape to the solution θ*. One Newton step with the exact Hessian H = JᵀJ + Σ r ∇²r is then taken from θ* (detached), with the gradient flowing through r and J. This step's derivative with respect to the inputs is the implicit-function gradient −H⁻¹ ∂(Jᵀr)/∂inputs at the optimum.

  • It was checked against finite differences of the converged solution (research R1).
  • Where H is not positive definite, the step falls back to Gauss–Newton.
  • Inference has no Newton step.

Training data​

Scenarios are generated on the fly from the training seed 310.

  • Noise draws. Each scenario gets two noise draws. Draw 0 is the generator's own bearings; draw 1 is truth + σ·N(0, 1), seeded by (seed, scenario, draw), with the scenario's own dropout pattern.
  • Scored rows. Each scenario contributes three per epoch: the gate rows zig + 5 and zig + 10 min, and one random post-zig row.
  • Batches and optimiser. Batches hold 64 scenarios. Adam is used with a learning rate of 1e-3 and gradient-norm clipping.
  • Determinism. Every random choice is a pure function of (seed, epoch, batch). The validation loss is the mean normalised squared error on fixed validation rows. Early stopping uses patience 4.

Export​

The whole model exports to one ONNX graph:

  • Inputs: bearing, valid, ox, oy and t of shape [B, 240], sigma [B], and k [B] as int64.
  • Outputs: state, cov, cov_ok and plausible.

The K iterations are a single ONNX Loop: the eager layer calls a TorchScript-scripted iteration function while tracing. The graph has about 1,500 nodes and loads in under a second. CI checks the graph against PyTorch in double in three runtimes, to 1e-5 (measured: 1.5e-9 in Python, 1.5e-9 in Java, 2.4e-9 in the browser).

Controls (spec 003 FR-017)​

  • (a) Direct head. The same encoder regresses the corrections and three log-variances at row k, with no physics layer. It is never carried.
  • (c) Shuffled labels. Truths are permuted within each batch. It runs through the physics layer, so it stays near least squares and cannot fall to naive level. It must score no better than least squares on the same rows (constitution 1.2.0; Gate 1 decision amendment 1).
  • (d) Time-late. An unmasked encoder and a solve over the whole window, reporting row k. It must score at least as well as the causal model.
