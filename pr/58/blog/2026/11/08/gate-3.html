Gate 3: E's stated uncertainty can be widened, but not made honest, by a scale

November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Gate 3 asked whether the carried Gate 2 estimator E can state an uncertainty that can be trusted. The answer on validation is no, and the gate stopped there: the calibration did not meet the pre-registered validation rule, so the fresh test seed was not generated.

E's raw covariance, from the physics solve, is badly overconfident: its 95% interval covers 0.60 of rows in log range and only a third of rows fall within the χ²₄ 95% point. A learned calibration can widen the stated standard deviations until 92–99% of each quantity is covered. It cannot make the joint 4-state statement honest (0.835 within the χ²₄ point, against 0.92 required), and it does no better than a classical per-slice temperature. The review panel found why, and the reasons point to a different design.

What was tested​

Every prediction row now states the covariance of (x, y, vx, vy) at the row and the standard deviations of log range, course and speed derived from it (see uncertainty calibration). On a validation baseline before any training, every estimator was overconfident except the oracle:

A calibration head rescales E's covariance without touching its points. It reads E's encoder state at the row, the raw standard deviations and C's code, and outputs four log-scales, for the standard deviations of log range, bearing, speed and course, keeping the stated correlations. It was trained on E's frozen features over the training stream, three seeds and a shuffled-label control. The classical control is a temperature: the same four scales fitted per cell of (time since the zig × transition open at the row).

The decision record fixed the criteria before training. Two changes came after the baseline and before any training. The owner replaced the original consistency check (median NEES near its χ²₄ value) with a tail check, because E's errors are heavy-tailed and the two original checks could not both pass. And the head's form moved from one scale to four local ones, because covering course needed its standard deviation widened about 30 times, range and speed only about 5. The decision forecast an overall pass at about 0.1, with the tail check the likely failure.

Why, according to the panel​

Five reviewers (statistics, numerics, TMA geometry, reproducibility, red team) approved the stop and corrected the account:

  • Course coverage is largely vacuous. For more than half the carried head's rows the 95% course interval covers the whole circle (median stated course sd 101°). Course coverage is reached by giving up on course, not by calibrating it. Course binds because it is the least observable state on short or low-bearing-rate legs and just after a zig.
  • The kept correlations sink the joint check. E's local covariance is near-singular (median correlation 0.81 between log range and speed). With the same scales and the correlations dropped, 0.957 of rows would fall within the χ²₄ point. A scale-only family cannot pass.
  • The loss is not the score. The head wins on its training loss and loses on the interval score: it widens log range everywhere to absorb tails that course drives. It trained on linearised errors, which cannot see a course flip, while the gate scores exact errors.
  • No per-row learning is shown. The head is close to a constant; moved by its best single factor, it is no better than its shuffled control.
  • Much of the failure is physical. On rows during or just after a manoeuvre even the oracle, with the true segmentation, falls well short (0.64 within the χ²₄ point): the bearings have not yet determined the new leg.

What follows​

Recommended follow-on (adopted if the owner has not objected by the next spec):

  1. Report course as undetermined when it is. A circular distribution for course, or an explicit "course not yet determined" state, instead of a Gaussian sd above 90°.
  2. Gate the uncertainty on observability. Calibrate and report on rows where the leg is determined, using a causal proxy (time since the last detected change, C's open-transition code), with a "manoeuvre suspected" flag otherwise.
  3. Shrink the stated correlations and train on the exact errors the gate scores.
  4. A heavy-tailed predictive only for the manoeuvre component, weighted by E's own change evidence.

The carried estimator stays E; its point estimates are unchanged. Its raw covariance should not be shown to an operator as an uncertainty. The validation record, the decision and the panel's reports are in experiments/20261108-g3-val.
