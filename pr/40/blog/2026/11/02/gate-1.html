Gate 1: the physics layer survives training, and the network learns nothing

November 2, 2026 · 5 min read

Gate 1 passed on constant-velocity data, but not for the reason the project hoped. The learned heads collapsed to constants during training, so the carried model is batch least squares computed by the physics layer. What Gate 1 shows is narrower and still worth having: end-to-end training through the declared physics layer is stable and does not degrade least squares at the information limit. Whether the learned component can add anything is untested until Gate 2.

The gate​

On a fresh test seed (303, 10,000 scenarios, generated once), σ = 1° rows at zig + 5 min (range, course) and zig + 10 min (speed), the carried model had to be within 1.1 × batch least squares on every median with the whole interval below, have a gross-error rate at or below least squares', and have efficiency (median error ÷ Cramér–Rao bound ÷ 0.6745) at most 1.25. All of it was fixed in a decision record committed before training (experiments/20261101-g1-decision/).

Beside it, on the same rows: least squares 3.33% median range error (90th percentile 14.5%; the model 14.4%), the tuned IMM 1.03 × least squares at the median and 1.17 × at the 90th percentile, the EKF 1.04 × and 1.20 ×, naive about 12 ×. The σ = 2° rows, not gated, tell the same story (90th percentile 29.2% against 29.6%). On constant-velocity data least squares is at the bound and the trackers' process noise costs them a little, as expected.

What the panel found​

Five independent reviewers (geometry, numerics, statistics, reproducibility, red team) approved with findings, and every one of the substantive findings points the same way:

  • The heads are dead. In the carried model every head ends with 0 of 64 hidden units active; the per-bearing weights are one constant, the start correction and covariance scale are their biases. On validation the trained model's estimate equals the same model with its heads zeroed (that is, untrained) to a median 3.5e-11 relative. Its test numbers equal, to every digit, those of the physics layer with trivial inputs (the "oracle" row, which on constant-velocity data has nothing to know). An untrained model would have passed.
  • The gross-error edge is a convergence flag. The 38 rows with a range error over 50% are the same for least squares, the model and the untrained layer. The whole margin is 4 rows where least squares' solver reported non-convergence and the physics layer did not (24 against 20; exact sign test p = 0.06): a difference in how the two define convergence, not in accuracy, and nothing to do with learning. The gross result reads "no worse than least squares", not "better". The decision record's forecast said the learned start would avoid wrong minima; that was wrong.
  • The validation stop that was not. The pre-registered shuffled-label control had to score "at naive level". Run through the physics layer it scores near least squares whatever it learns, so the registered rule would have stopped the phase at validation. The owner amended the rule (constitution 1.2.0) after training started and before the validation report; the amendment and this consequence are recorded.

None of this changes the verdict under the registered rules, and all of it changes what the verdict means.

Why the heads died​

On constant-velocity data with Gaussian noise, uniform weights and the least squares start are optimal, so the loss gives the heads nothing to do; the untrained model's validation loss (3.27 per row, against 3.0 at the bound) is already at the floor, and it stayed there for every seed. The heads' last layers start at zero by design, so the model starts as least squares; with nothing to gain, the hidden ReLU layers drifted dead and the encoder's output collapsed. That is a legitimate optimum here and a problem for Gate 2: a dead ReLU never revives.

What changes before Gate 2​

  • No warm start from these weights; head activations that cannot die; a test of alive units and of how much head outputs vary across rows.
  • A learnability check before any Gate 2 plan, on data where the heads must matter (outlier or heteroscedastic bearings, or the manoeuvring data), so a dead network cannot pass silently again.
  • Weights bound by digest in predictions and gate records; gross errors reported by cause; one median convention for efficiency; a minimum improvement in early stopping.

Caveats​

  • Zig + 5 min falls inside the second ownship turn for about two thirds of the rows; these numbers are not comparable with gitma's single-zig results.
  • Efficiency is median-based; least squares itself scores below 1 on course and speed, which reflects heavy tails rather than beating the bound, so the 1.25 limit separates little this close to it.
  • The direct-regression control was under-trained (validation loss 50.5 → 50.0); its 7–12 × is not a fair measure of direct regression.
