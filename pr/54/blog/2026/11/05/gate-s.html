Gate S: the classical segmenter carries, learning is not shown

November 5, 2026 · 8 min read

Gate S asked one learning question. Does a learned segmenter, L, find the target's steady legs better than the classical segmenter, C, that it is built on? On the fresh test seed the answer is no measurable difference: L minus C in gap recovered is +0.0012, with a 97.5% interval of [−0.0031, +0.0049]. The pre-registered rule counts an inconclusive S4 as not passed. So C carries into Gate 2, and learning is recorded as not shown.

The classical result is strong. C recovers 0.90 of the range-error gap between a one-leg fit and an oracle that knows the true change times. The GLR + confirm interim segmenter recovers 0.14.

The agent review panel's first round rejected the first record: C read later bearings on a few percent of prefixes. The bug was fixed and everything the gate depends on was re-made; the verdicts did not change, and the panel approved the corrected record in its second round.

What was tested​

SRD Annex A §A.9 defines three components on the owner's manoeuvre model (generator 0.6.0, MAN2):

  • O, the ownship segmenter, from ownship's own course and speed;
  • C, the classical target segmenter: GLR alarms and an additive leg search nominate transitions, and the coupled physics-layer fit on each nomination's own prefix confirms them (see steady-leg segmentation);
  • L, which is C plus learned per-bearing evidence. Untrained, L is C, byte for byte.

L's evidence network is a small causal convolution. It was trained on 10,000 scenarios from stream seed 317 by the semi-Markov likelihood of the true segmentation, with three seeds and a shuffled-label control. On validation no seed beat C. The median seed (seed 3) was frozen by SHA-256 before the test split was opened.

The decision record fixed the four criteria, the limits, the carried-seed rule, the shuffled rule, the follow-on and a forecast before any L training.

The forecast was L − C of +0.01 [−0.01, +0.03], most likely inconclusive. The result is nearer zero and tighter than forecast. Constitution IV allows one re-score of an inconclusive result on a larger fresh seed; it is not taken. The decision record fixed before any result that inconclusive counts as not passed, and S4 has no minimum effect size, so a seed ten times larger could turn +0.001 into a "pass" that would change nothing in practice.

Reading it​

  • C's lead over GLR + confirm is in its segmentation. Handing GLR + confirm's knots to the physics layer as C's are handed over (gap mode) recovers 0.108 against 0.143 with its own hand-over; on the rows every estimator answers it helps a little (0.276 against 0.263). Either way the hand-over explains at most about 0.02 of a margin near 0.6. What C adds is the second nominator, transition modelling and the coupled confirmation, and above all it rarely fails: 0.4% of rows have no plausible estimate, against a fifth for GLR + confirm.
  • Read the 0.90 with care. It is measured from one leg's median, and one leg, like the IMM, fails outright on a quarter of the rows; even the naive baseline has a lower median. On the 14,142 rows every estimator answers, C recovers about 0.84 of the gap (0.838; 0.837 measured from the naive baseline instead of one leg). On MAN2's long manoeuvring windows the references are weak, and C is still 2.7 times the bound.
  • Why L adds nothing. Two explanations fit, and they are not exclusive.
    • Little headroom. C leaves 0.10 of the gap. By detectability band, C's gap recovered runs from 0.76 on the weakest changes to 0.96 on the strongest; much of what is left is in changes that bearings at σ = 1° barely reveal.
    • The design and its training limit L. L's loss teaches the additive search's lattice, but C also takes GLR nominations (the share from each was not counted), where the evidence can only shift a capped threshold. And one validation prefix with a failed fit in the search's cost table dominated the validation loss, so early stopping ended two of the three seeds too soon. The best-trained seed is no better than C on validation either, and the evidence barely separates steady from transition rows.

Where L and C differ at all is on rows that end inside an open transition: on the 18,481 rows where neither is open their gap recovered is identical (0.9066 against 0.9065). Those open rows are C's weakest (0.66 against 0.91 elsewhere), because an open transition is handed over with a knot at the scored row, and they are the main lever for Gate 2.

So the result says that this evidence network, trained this way, added nothing measurable. It does not say that learning cannot help segmentation.

What the panel found​

Five reviewers (geometry, numerics, statistics, reproducibility, red team) reviewed the first record. The red team rejected it:

  • A causality bug (blocker). When C closed an open transition it polished the start with bearings up to about six minutes later, and prefixes before the close reported that start: 5.8% of C's test rows and 4.7% of L's were exposed. Fixed; every segmentation the gate depends on was re-made on CI from a clean tree, validation's rules were applied again (same mode, same carried seed, same shuffled-rule outcome), and the test split was re-scored. The verdicts are unchanged.
  • Missing reporting. The naive baseline, gross-error rates, efficiency and the required strata were not in the first record; they are now.
  • S2's margin could have been the hand-over. It is not (above).
  • L's training was cut short by an outlier, and the search's cost table has failed fits: both are recorded; the second is fixed before Gate 2.

The full findings and responses are in review.md.

Caveats​

  • S3's false-split check rests on 67 change-free test prefixes from 49 scenarios; both segmenters split none, and an exact bound on that count would be about 0.085, above the 0.05 limit.
  • The frozen trackers were tuned on validation rows that include their scoring rows. The gate's criteria are differences between segmentations and do not depend on them.
  • An open transition is handed over with a second knot at the scored row; the oracle uses only its start knot. Gate 2 must fix one convention.
  • L's training jobs recorded git_dirty because the workflow put downloaded tables and checkpoints inside the checkout; the code was clean, and the workflow is fixed.

What follows​

Fixed in advance (FR-072): C is Gate 2's segmenter, with the search's cost-table fix. Gate 2 reports its "half the gap to the oracle" for C as well, so a Gate 2 gain is credited to the right component. Because C already recovers 0.90 of the one-leg-to-oracle gap, Gate 2's decision record must say which gap it gates before any result exists.

Owner decision (5 November 2026): a learned nominator, or evidence trained through the confirmation step, is tested within Gate 2, not as a separate gate.

Record: experiments/20261105-gs-r2/ (the first record, experiments/20261105-gs/, is kept as reviewed).
