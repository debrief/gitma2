The time-late track as a whole: what six studies established, and what they did not

November 15, 2026 · 13 min read

Between 11 and 15 November the project ran six studies on the time-late consumer: the analyst who wants the target's whole track over a period after the fact, not the best estimate now. Each has its own write-up and its own panel review. This is the account of all six together: what is standing, what was shown, what was withdrawn, and what was never tested. Figures are from the committed test records and their panel reviews; the table of record is generated by a script. The illustrated companion is the slides post.

The question​

Given every bearing of a period of 20 to 120 minutes, at 5-second intervals, say where the target was, its course and its speed at every moment, and when it manoeuvred. Nothing was trained except in one study, which says so. Every result is on simulated data from one generator.

What is standing​

The classical control (study L's ctl, declared):

  1. wild bearings are edited out;
  2. the bearing sd is enlarged when the bearings' second differences show the errors are correlated in time;
  3. a search over manoeuvre histories, each fitted with an arc model (a turn at a constant rate, a speed change at a constant rate) and scored by its evidence under Annex B's manoeuvre statistics and under a broadened set, pooled;
  4. a manoeuvre is declared only when it is worth an evidence margin, with a stated 1% chance that the target never manoeuvres.

It replaced "C″ read as a track": the causal segmenter's answer at the end of the period, read backwards.

Its known faults, which a reader should have before trusting it:

  • it calls a one-manoeuvre target quiet more often than the search it grew from: 0.29 against 0.05 with correlated errors, 0.17 against 0.04 with 5% wild bearings;
  • on busier targets it misses about half the manoeuvres;
  • it does nothing under a slowly varying bias;
  • its editing and its enlarged sd know the simulator's error family (wild bearings at 10 sd are edited at 4), and its correlation estimate fires in 8% of clean periods;
  • the evidence it ranks by has an unrepaired fault at the edges of the prior's support;
  • the prior it uses is the simulator's own for Annex B targets.

What was shown​

All ratios are paired per period, in period median range error, on fresh test seeds, unless a line says otherwise.

The search with the arc model and Annex B's statistics roughly halves the old system's error. C″ ÷ search is 1.7 and 2.1 on Annex B targets in the gate and its follow-on (specs 013 and 014), and 2.05 and 2.24 for the repaired search on two later seeds; on busier targets 1.5 to 1.65; 3.1 with 2% of bearings wild. The panels narrowed what that means. It is mostly smoothing with the right motion model and prior, not finding the right history: the search wins where its history is wrong too, it is worse than C″ in about 30% of periods, and refitting C″'s own manoeuvres with the arc model and prior obtains between a third and a half of the gain (specs 014 and 015). A search under a flat prior obtains about four fifths of it: Annex B's statistics themselves are worth about a fifth.

Editing and an honest bearing sd remove most of the damage from bad bearings. Search ÷ control: 1.68 with 2% wild bearings, 2.44 with 5%, 1.33 with correlated errors, 1.70 on targets that hold course (false manoeuvres in 8% of periods against 49%). The outlier and correlated-error gains replicate over a tuning, a validation and a test seed. On clean Annex B targets the control is level with the search or slightly ahead (0.96 on validation; 1.05 [0.99, 1.12] and 1.09 [1.02, 1.18] on two test seeds; the median period is level), and under a slowly varying bias it does nothing (1.00).

Against the old system the standing control is 2.1 to 2.4 times better on Annex B targets, 1.6 to 1.7 on busier targets, about 5 with wild bearings, 2.9 with correlated errors, and level on targets that hold course (seeds 344 and 347).

What remains, against a fit told the true manoeuvre history, depends on how that fit is made. The undeclared control against a reference whose times are free unless it drifts: 1.3 on Annex B targets, 1.2 on targets with larger, rarer manoeuvres, 1.8 to 2.1 on busier targets (three seeds). The declared control against a reference with its times held: 1.6, 1.5 and 2.3 to 2.7 (two seeds). These references are told true times and are not bounds: the control beats them in a quarter of periods on busier targets and in up to nearly a half elsewhere.

On busier targets part of the remainder cannot be closed, and how much depends on where the line is drawn. Over a third of their manoeuvres add less than 20 to the fit's χ² when left out singly (16% on Annex B targets); that single-omission measure is not additive and overstates what is hidden. A reference told only the manoeuvres above the line leaves the control 0.65, 0.44 or 0.14 of its gap to close at lines of 10, 20 and 40; more in course and speed than in range. The closable part sits in the under-a-third of periods where the control misses a manoeuvre the bearings show (spec 017).

Beyond range. The first candidate history is the true one in about 0.64 of Annex B periods. The candidates' stated probabilities are honest on average on Annex B targets and carry only a rank on busier ones. In course and speed the control's gain over the old system is of the same size as in range; for the challengers below, course and speed are given where they differ.

  • The learned model's declared read-out lost in the conditions it was trained on, and won only on targets that hold course (1.06). The panel showed the registered comparison measured its untuned manoeuvre detector: the median period is a tie, and the loss sits where the model declares fewer manoeuvres. Its own track leans on what targets do in general, winning where the bearings say little and losing by three where they say much. "This model with this read-out stops"; a learned proposer inside the search was not tested.
  • The right prior is worth about a tenth to a sixth on busier targets, and not in the typical period. On seed 347 it is better than the control in 53% of periods, over twice as good in 20% and over twice as bad in 13%: two heavy tails that nearly cancel. (Spec 018's record says ten periods of 355 carry 87% of the gain. That statistic drops only the best periods and reads any widely spread ratio as a tail; trimmed at both ends the ratio is unchanged at 1.10.)
  • This evidence, on one period, does not choose among these 30 priors. It does not rank them by accuracy; choosing by groups of periods does no better. The family's cost on Annex B targets is the design's: it lacked Annex B's own prior, which the control has, and its grid was set knowing the test targets' statistics. That a prior cannot be adapted from one period in general was not shown.
  • A hedged mean is not better than a declared history, and its spread could not be stated by one factor.

The last diagnostic​

Spec 018's panel left one thing confounded: the family of priors only refitted a shared set of candidate histories, and it lacked parts of the true statistics. One run on 130 periods already used (seed 347; development work, not a result; nothing in it is separated from 1 in range) looks at both.

  • Refitting was not the handicap. The family member nearest the truth, given a search of its own, is 1.03 [0.91, 1.19] of the control; refitting shared candidates on the same periods it was 1.03 [0.91, 1.19].
  • The gain builds along the ladder, legs first. Taking on the truth's legs, turn sizes, turn rates, speed changes and kinds of manoeuvre in that order gives 0.98, 1.05, 1.06, 1.12, 1.10 and 1.11 [0.94, 1.32]. The first three parts reach the whole prior's figure in range. The other four parts each alone on the broadened prior, without the legs, do nothing (0.92 to 1.00). One order only; a direction, not a decomposition.
  • Neither typical nor a tail. The true prior is better than the control in 55% of these periods, over twice as good in a quarter and over twice as bad in an eighth; trimmed of its ten best and ten worst periods the ratio is 1.14.
  • What the right prior changes steadily is the count: manoeuvres missed fall from 0.52 to 0.36 down the ladder, with false ones near a half a period throughout. In course and speed it is further ahead (1.21 and 1.24, not quite separated).

So the family was not held back by how it was scored. It was short of the truth mainly in how often these targets manoeuvre and how they turn, which it varied, and it still could not be chosen by one period's evidence.

What was withdrawn or corrected along the way​

The record matters as much as the results. In six studies:

  • Spec 013's first reading of the manoeuvre prior was withdrawn: the evidence had been evaluated at a bearings-only fit, which produced thousands of units of artefact. The owner's decision made on that reading went back to the owner.
  • Gate T stopped at validation the first time: its true-history reference started at the truth and stayed there, scoring better than the bound. It was re-registered with a reference fitted from neutral starts.
  • The evidence was wrong five times: evaluated at a bearings-only fit; three small errors in the prior's density; a manoeuvre duration's volume (repaired in spec 015); and the edges of the prior's support (recorded, unrepaired, and applying to every system since). Separately, the rule for manoeuvres cut by a period's edge was wrong in the classing of histories and was repaired in spec 015.
  • Forecasts that were wrong: the share of the search's gain that refitting obtains (0.75 forecast and 0.43 measured in spec 014; 0.50 forecast and 0.33 measured in spec 015); spec 017's forecast that the control would not be separated from the detectable-only reference (it was, though the test value lay inside the tuning interval); spec 018's forecast of a trade. Forecasts read off one tuning run were guesses each time.
  • Rules that measured the wrong thing: spec 016's comparison (a detector, not a model); spec 017's second condition (counts, not cost); spec 018's spread band (its edge sat on the tuning value).
  • Statistics that misled: ratios of medians read as typical gains (spec 013); a gain "carried by its ten best periods" (spec 018 and this spec's first draft), which is what any widely spread ratio looks like when only its best cases are dropped.
  • Words the panels took back: "ceiling", "bound", "upper limit" and "lower limit" for fits told the truth; "ambiguous" for a property of the Laplace evidence; "harm" for a tail with a level median.

What was never tested, and what was tested and left unremedied​

Never tested:

  • a learned proposer inside the search, training through the arc model, a larger training budget, or the control and the learned model's own track combined;
  • a longer history of the same target than one period, to learn its habits;
  • advice to ownship: where the bearings do not show a manoeuvre, ownship's own motion is what would;
  • a family of priors that includes Annex B's own; a working diagnostic that the prior is wrong;
  • the owner's bearing-rate thinning idea (the adaptive rule built for it broke its bins on noise, so the idea itself is untested);
  • a whitened fit or a Huber cost in place of editing and an enlarged sd; a smoothed IMM as a reference; a spread for course and speed; real data of any kind.

Tested and left unremedied: a slowly varying bias (the control does nothing); the evidence's fault at the edges of the prior's support. Thinly tested: whole engagements, on only 60 periods in each of three studies, and the control itself on 60.

What it adds to the project's question​

The project asks whether a learned component can match the classical solution where the simple motion model holds and beat it where it does not. On the time-late consumer the answer is "not shown, at a small budget, with one direct model and one untuned read-out"; as a proposer inside the search a learned model is untested. The large gains came from the motion model (how a manoeuvre unfolds) and from handling bad bearings; the manoeuvre statistics added about a fifth. What is left on targets unlike Annex B's is partly manoeuvres the bearings do not show, and the closable part resisted one learned model and two classical attempts.

A learned approach would be worth trying again if one of three things changed: the data (real bearings, whose errors and target habits no simulator prior states exactly); the information (several periods of the same target, or ownship manoeuvres chosen to reveal it); or the role (a proposer whose suggestions the evidence still judges). None is in the SRD's present scope without the owner's decision.
