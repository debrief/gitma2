The steady-leg detector

Sources:

  • src/tma2/models/ (detector, detector_labels, detector_inputs, detector_loss, detector_train, detector_predict, detector_export);
  • src/tma2/detect/ (controls, matching, downstream) and src/tma2/eval/gd.py (Gate D).

Requirements: SRD Annex A and spec 004. The physics layer consumes the detector's output.

The detector answers one question about the target's track: where its velocity changed, and how. It does not estimate the target's state. Its outputs become the physics layer's knots and its speed-continuity prior. The estimate is the physics layer's, with trivial per-bearing weights, so the detector is credited only for segmentation.

Inputs​

One window has R = 360 rows (60 min at 10 s). Each row has 13 channels, every one with a fixed scale, so no statistic is taken from the data:

  • the bearing as sin z · v and cos z · v, and the valid flag v;
  • ownship position relative to the first row, ÷ 10 km;
  • ownship course as sin and cos, and ownship speed ÷ 10 m/s;
  • time ÷ 1 h;
  • the window mask;
  • the trailing least-squares bearing rate over 18 rows (gitma's derived channel, ÷ 10⁻³ rad/s), with its validity flag;
  • the bearing-line intercept oₓ cos z − o_y sin z, ÷ 10 km.

The features are computed inside the exported graph.

Encoder and heads​

The encoder is the Gate 1 model's: a linear embedding to width 64, two pre-norm transformer blocks with four heads, and a layer norm. The causal variant masks future rows. The time-late variant sees the whole window and is asked its question at the last row.

On the encoder output h, with one vector per row:

  • p_change[k] = σ(MLP(h_k)) is the probability that a manoeuvre is in progress at k or began within the last 60 s.
  • w_change[k, j] = σ(b + (Q h_k)·(K h_j)/√d) for 0 < j ≤ k is a soft changepoint weight at row j, as judged from row k. The asking row k has seen the bearings after j, which is where a course change becomes visible.
  • p_kind[k, j] has the same bilinear form. It is the probability that a changepoint at j is a course change rather than a speed change.

Every MLP is Linear → tanh → zero-initialised Linear; tanh cannot die, as Gate 1's ReLU heads did. In each pairwise head the key projection starts at zero and b starts at −10. So the untrained detector gives p_change = p_kind = 0.5 and w_change ≈ 4.5·10⁻⁵ everywhere. It exports no knot, and the physics layer then reduces to batch least squares.

From segmentation to the physics layer​

At a scored row k:

  • the layer's changepoint input is w_change[k, :], and it keeps the 8 largest weights as knots;
  • the speed-continuity weight and its skip at knot j are both p_kind[k, j]. A course change ties the speed after the knot to the speed before the previous one.

Research R4 measured this mapping on truth boundaries: 0.442 × batch least squares' median range error on G5 rows, against the exact oracle's 0.435.

The exported knots are the local maxima of w_change[k, :] at or above 0.5, the 8 largest. A plateau counts once, at its first row, so a one-row speed change gives one knot. A knot with p_kind ≥ 0.5 is a course change.

Labels and losses​

Labels come from the stored manoeuvre spans; both the start and the end of a span are boundaries.

  • now[k] is 1 when a span is in progress at k or began within 60 s.
  • The pairwise target is a triangular kernel of ±60 s around each boundary at or before k.
  • The kind target is that of the nearest past boundary.

The loss has four terms, each with weight 1:

  • binary cross-entropy for now, with held-leg rows weighted 3;
  • binary cross-entropy for the pairwise boundaries;
  • binary cross-entropy for kind, weighted by the boundary target;
  • the downstream loss.

The downstream loss is the primary objective. It runs the physics layer, fed the soft segmentation, at two scored rows per scenario: one G5 row and one random post-zig row. Its error in log range, vx and vy is divided by the known-manoeuvres Cramér–Rao bound (a numba port, 0.4 ms per scenario), squared and capped. Gradients reach the detector through the implicit Newton step. A detector trained without this term is the declared control.

Controls​

Gate D compares the detector with three detectors that an engineer would build without learning. Each threshold is tuned on validation to the detector's own held-leg false-alarm rate.

  • CUSUM on the per-timestep least-squares innovations, as Annex A writes it. Because the one-leg fit is wrong after the first manoeuvre, it is weak on manoeuvring data: it detects 10% of spans at a 5% false-alarm rate. A CUSUM on the EKF's innovations is reported beside it.
  • GLR: a velocity change in the last 30 samples, linearised at the per-timestep least-squares estimate. The window's one-leg fit is projected out, so only a change inside the window counts. It detects 57% of spans at 5%.
  • The IMM's turn-mode probability μ_CT from the frozen reference.

Export​

The detector exports alone to ONNX: double precision, a 360-row window, dynamic batch, and no Loop or If. Python onnxruntime, onnxruntime-web (WASM) and Java agree with torch to 1e-13. WASM takes 0.10 s per window, against Annex A's one-second limit; CI checks this.
