Steady-leg segmentation: C, O and L

Source: src/tma2/segment/; CLIs tma2-segment, tma2-segtrain and tma2-gs. Requirements: SRD Annex A §A.9 (Gate S), Annex B (the owner's manoeuvre definitions) and spec 005.

The problem is to label, at each scored row k and using bearings up to k only, every earlier row of the target's track as steady, transition or unknown. The labels then go to the physics layer, which fits one constant-velocity leg per steady piece. Three components do the labelling:

  • O labels ownship legs from navigation data.
  • C is the classical target segmenter.
  • L is C with learned per-bearing evidence added.

Definitions (Annex B)​

  • Steady leg: at least L_min = 30 rows (5 min), with course within ± 5° and speed within ± 0.5 kt of the leg mean.
  • Transition: at least T_min = 6 rows (60 s). Its length is estimated.
  • Unknown: rows the evidence cannot decide. For the target, every row up to the ownship's first zig is unknown: before it, one leg cannot be told from two.

Truth labels come from the generator's manoeuvre spans, widened to T_min. A steady piece shorter than L_min becomes unknown.

O: ownship legs​

A coarse pass compares 1-min moving averages of course and speed with D = 10° and D_S = 1 kt to find deliberate changes. A fine walk then finds each transition's edges:

  • it starts from the steepest row and walks outward;
  • it stops where the rate changes sign, or where the track enters the neighbouring leg's ± 5° band.

A change is kept only if the change in leg means exceeds D and a signed test across the transition rows confirms it. Thirty seconds are trimmed from each leg end. On MAN2, O finds every ownship turn within 30 s with zero contamination; helm wander and autopilot oscillation call no transition.

C: nominate, then confirm by the coupled cost​

  1. Nominators.
    • GLR alarms. GLR is the spec 004 control, on per-timestep batch least squares. Its threshold is tuned on validation to a 20% held-leg exceedance; a raised alarm is re-tested every 6 rows.
    • An additive search over legs, each scored by its own constant-velocity fit. It is run on prefixes every 30 rows, on a 6-row grid anchored at the scored rows, so one cost table serves every prefix. A per-leg penalty of 30 holds false transitions to 3.5% of prefixes.
  2. Confirmation.
    • The test. Each nomination is tested by the physics-layer solve on its own prefix, a coupled fit with knots at both transition ends.
    • Search order. Starts are searched at the middle transition length, then every length is tried at the best start.
    • Acceptance. An open transition (start knot only) is accepted if its cost drop exceeds τ = 13.8, the 0.1% point of χ² with 2 degrees of freedom; a closed one (two knots) against the 4-degree-of-freedom point with the same tail probability, 18.45. Both boundaries are then polished by ± 3 rows.
    • Causality. Closing an open transition polishes its start with bearings up to the closing row, so prefixes before the close keep the start as it was accepted (FR-002; the Gate S panel found the start being rewritten for earlier prefixes, fixed in 7fe8874).
  3. Segmentation of each prefix. Accepted transitions become transitions, near misses become unknown, and the rest is steady.

The search alone is a weak nominator on MAN2: it finds 13% of changes within ± 60 s. C's accepted transitions come from both nominators; the share from each has not been counted.

Hand-over to the physics layer: gap or chord​

A transition [s, e] gives the physics layer knots at s and e. An open transition at prefix k is [s, k], so it gets its second knot, and in chord mode the speed prior, at k itself; the oracle and confirmation instead treat an open span as a start knot only. Either way the state at k is weakly determined. Gate 2 must fix one convention for both. A segmenter reports no kinds, so the speed prior is applied across every transition, speed changes included.

  • Gap: the bearings inside the transition are excluded.
  • Chord: the bearings are kept, with the speed-continuity prior across a course-only transition.

The mode is fixed on validation: gap had the lower median range error, 0.120 against 0.123.

Known defect (Gate S panel round 2). An open transition reserves only [s, s + 1] when later nominations are checked for overlap, so a second transition can be accepted inside the first's eventual span, and the close picks its length without an overlap check: the two then merge into one interval (21 of C's 1,148 open test intervals are older than the 35-row close; one reaches 60 rows). Polish checks only T_min, so polished transitions reach 42 rows. Rare, the same for C and L. Fixed after Gate S: an open transition now reserves its longest span, [s, s + l_max − 1]; the close takes only lengths that keep clear of other transitions; polish keeps a transition within T_min … l_max rows (test_segment_c_fixes.py).

Known defect (Gate S panel round 1). The search's cost table warm-starts each leg's fit from the shorter leg's and takes a fresh grid start only every fourth step, so some entries are failed fits: a longer leg costs less than a nested shorter one (by more than 100 χ² for 3,191 pairs in 500 validation scenarios). It weakens the search nominator for C and L alike. Fixed after Gate S: a warm-started fit whose cost jumps by more than 9 χ² per added bearing plus 30 is re-tried from a grid start, and a backward pass re-fits each shorter leg from its longer extension's solution where the order is broken, keeping the lower cost. On 40 validation scenarios the order violations above 1 χ² fell from 991 to 0 and 7,335 entries came down; 67 in 36,000 rose (by up to 40 χ²: the changed warm-start chain found a worse local minimum); the tables take about 1.6 times as long.

Open-transition hand-over. A parameter since Gate S (open_handover): knot_at_k (spec 005's), start_masked (a start knot, the bearings from s to k excluded) or start_chord (a start knot, the bearings kept). It is chosen on validation before Gate 2. Absent optional parameters are left out of the parameter digest, so Gate S's digests are unchanged.

Code identity. Each segmentation header records code_sha256, a digest of the source files tma2-segment runs (segment/codeid.py), so a gate can refuse segmentations made by other code.

Reproducing a segmentation. The physics-layer solves are batched 16 scenarios at a time and padded to the longest; the padding changes the last bits of the costs, which can flip a near-threshold acceptance. A scenario re-run alone can therefore differ from its CI segmentation; re-run with the plan's ordinals and shards.

Scoring conventions. Contamination counts a segmenter's unknown rows as steady, and a truth transition [start_index, end_index] includes the first row on the new leg. Both are conservative and the same for every segmenter.

L: learned evidence​

  • The network. A small causal dilated 1-D convolution: width 32, kernel 5, dilations 1, 2, 4 and 8, SiLU activations and a zero-initialised head.
  • Its inputs:
    • the bearing;
    • ownship position and time;
    • O's online label: for each row r, O run on rows 0 … r alone;
    • the per-timestep least-squares innovation.
  • Its output. For each row, a log-likelihood ratio e of steady against transition.
  • Where e enters C:
    • the search's costs, as −2Σe on legs and +2Σe on transitions;
    • the confirmation threshold, which shifts by twice e summed over the candidate's rows, capped at ± τ/2.

An untrained L emits e = 0 and equals C exactly.

Training minimises the semi-Markov negative log-likelihood of the truth segmentation on the search's lattice (segment/crf.py), plus 0.1 × a per-bearing cross-entropy term.

  • The normaliser is a log-sum-exp forward recursion over every segmentation the lattice allows.
  • The truth path is the lattice segmentation that agrees with the most labelled rows, with ties broken by the cost.
  • The shuffled control trains on another scenario's labels.

It exports to ONNX with each convolution written as matrix products, because onnxruntime has no double-precision Conv.

Measures​

  • Contamination: the share of a reported leg's rows that the truth labels as transition.
  • Coverage: the share of truth-steady rows inside reported legs.
  • False split: any transition at a prefix where no target change has begun by its row k (the measure as built, segment/metrics.py; on a scenario with no change at all, every prefix counts).
  • Gap recovered: (median one-leg error − median segmentation error) ÷ (median one-leg error − median oracle error), on σ = 1° G5 rows. One leg scores 0 and the oracle 1.

Every measure is stratified by detectability, the oracle's cost drop for each change (note 08's bands: below 13.3, 13.3–50, 50–200, at least 200).
