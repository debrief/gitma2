The long-engagement track as a whole: what seven studies established, and what they did not

November 21, 2026 · 12 min read

The long-engagement track asked whether the project's time-late system holds up over engagements of hours rather than one two-hour period, and whether carrying information from one window to the next makes it better. Seven studies, all in one simulator, give these answers:

  • Stitched 2-hour windows held up on the tended contact. The registered study covered 6 to 12 hours. Beyond that there is one engagement each at 24 and 48 hours, which are anecdotes.
  • A carried start is not shown to help accuracy. It saves about 31% of the search where it runs, and on its own it can cost accuracy.
  • A carried prior beats the same window alone where that window cannot see range.
    • On 1-hour windows it reads about 1.7 to 2.4.
    • On 2-hour windows it reads 1.54 on one seed and 1.37 pooled over three, all of it beyond 35 km.
    • Against a joint solve over the same past bearings it is not shown to be better.

The owner adopted 2-hour ctl-prior on 2026-11-21. This post closes the track unless the owner reopens it.

Spec: specs/034-long-synthesis/. Figures of record: experiments/20261121-long-synthesis/tables.md, generated from the test records by scripts/research/long_synthesis.py. Two reviewers checked this account against the records before it was published: experiments/20261121-long-synthesis/review.md.

The question​

Real engagements last hours. The project's time-late system, ctl (the evidence search with a declared history, from the time-late track), works on one period of up to two hours. The track asked three things:

  • whether ctl on overlapping windows, stitched together, keeps its accuracy over 6 to 48 hours;
  • what the real-time systems and the IMM do over the same spans;
  • whether information carried from one window into the next (a start, then a prior) improves the next window.

Everything here comes from one simulator (src/tma2/long/sim.py) with Annex B targets. Spec 023's ownship steered on the true target. From spec 024 on, a realism layer added steering on measured bearings with a ±30% range belief, gaps, detection that falls to 50% at 40 km, and a second contact. Those realism settings were chosen by hand, not measured. Nothing was trained.

What is standing​

ctl on 2-hour windows overlapping 30 minutes, stitched mid-overlap (spec 023). This is the time-late system for long engagements, and the control in every later study.

  • Latency: a window answers only when it closes, so each estimate comes up to 2 hours after the earliest bearing it uses.
  • Manoeuvre cap: ctl declares at most ten manoeuvres per window.

2-hour ctl-prior for the tended contact, up to 12 hours (adopted by the owner, 2026-11-21). This is ctl with the previous window's state and covariance carried in as a Gaussian prior. The prior is widened (scale 4, tail 1,200 s) and guarded by a χ²(4) test at α 0.001 (spec 028). Its faults stand beside its gains:

  • Where the gain is. On blind 2-hour windows the gain is a far-range effect: about 2.2 beyond 35 km and about 1.0 under.
    • It was read on the half of blind windows where contact held: 85 of 165, with 80 lost to contact.
    • Range and contact loss move together in this design, so they cannot be separated.
    • The prior was handed on from a window containing an ownship turn in 83 of 85 cases.
    • Across seeds it read 1.32, 0.87 and 1.54.
    • It was decided on contact 1 only, in one simulator.
  • The carried covariance is not calibrated. It is too wide in the core and too narrow in the tail, and covers 0.79 across an ownship turn, so it must not be shown as an uncertainty.
  • The guard rarely fires: 1.8% of guarded windows in spec 028, none of the blind windows in spec 032.
  • Known defect: the hand-over refit starts from the full-window fit, so it can keep overlap information. Spec 028 recorded this and did not repair it.
  • Placement: every chained study placed its windows past true target manoeuvres, so no hand-over ever fell inside one.
  • Cost: the job that runs ctl-prior alongside ctl, ctl-carry and a no-guard variant cost about 190–320 CPU-seconds per engagement-hour (specs 031 and 032). The cost of ctl-prior alone was not separated.

What was shown​

Feasibility: specs 023 and 024​

These ran one engagement per length, so they are anecdotes, not results.

  • Stitched ctl held 1.6–2.7% median range error at 6, 12, 24 and 48 hours, with no penalty at the seams.
  • The real-time systems (the carried model E and C″) sat at 13–23%.
  • The IMM degraded over 48 hours and answered 81% of evaluations.
  • With spec 024's realism, the tended contact stayed at 1.5–2.2%. The untended contact (the one the ownship does not steer for) kept only 10–22% of its bearings beyond 6 hours. Where it was held, ctl was at about 6–8%.
  • Run time: one ctl period took 1 minute at 30 minutes long and 114 minutes at 6 hours, which is why the windows are 2 hours. A real-time estimate cost 27–37 s.
  • An incremental C was 1.7× cheaper and less accurate, so it was not adopted.

Read together​

A carried start (ctl-carry) is not shown to help accuracy, and it saves search time.

  • Non-inferiority rests on ties. Spec 026's result rests partly on each chain's first window, which equals ctl by construction. Without those windows it is 1.021 [0.9502, 1.100].

  • The saving: on the windows that had a carried start, the search took 5.84 hours against 8.50 from nothing, a 31% saving. Over all windows that is about 7%.

  • Where it leans the other way:

    • the untended contact, 0.942 (0.920 without first windows);
    • spec 028, ctl ÷ ctl-carry 0.952 [0.893, 1.021] on contact 1;
    • spec 029, 0.89 [0.82, 0.97] on one-turn 1-hour windows.

    So a start is not used without the prior.

A carried prior beats the same window alone where that window cannot see range.

  • On 1-hour windows without an ownship turn: about 1.7 to 2.4. That is spec 029's 2.41, and 1.71 on spec 031's fresh seed.
  • On 2-hour windows the ownship cannot turn in: 1.54 on spec 032's test seed, 1.37 [1.05, 1.80] pooled over three seeds. It is about 2.2 beyond 35 km and about 1.0 under, and the median window is 1.25.
  • Where the window can see range, it is a small average gain with many losses:
    • spec 028's contact-1 windows read 1.11 [1.006, 1.22], median 1.03, which its panel called "an average shift with many losses";
    • one-turn windows read 1.22 and 1.26.
  • Every one of these is against the same window alone. Both panels called that "more data beats no data".

Spec 028's weak split was not the owner's case. Spec 028 was registered to test the blind-window case, but the clause that should have found windows without a useful ownship turn never fired: with turns every 20–40 minutes, a 2-hour window is never blind in this simulator. The decided weak split was in fact the untended contact plus contact-1 windows near a gap. Clustered by engagement, its interval is [0.951, 1.279] (spec 028's panel).

The prior is a summary of past bearings, and it is not shown to beat having them.

  • On 1-hour windows it lost to a joint solve over the same past bearings (spec 031): 0.774, median 0.78. ctl-prior was better in about 4 windows in 10.
  • On 2-hour windows it was not separated from a 3.5-hour joint solve (spec 032): 1.11, or 1.02 with engagements weighted equally.
  • Its practical case is a compact hand-over: four numbers and a covariance in place of hours of bearings.

2-hour windows beat 1-hour ctl-prior, but not on equal terms (spec 031).

  • Both 2-hour ctl (0.730) and 2-hour ctl-prior (0.488) beat it.
  • The stitched 2-hour rows include an ownship turn in 98% of windows and a median of 29 minutes of bearings after the hour.
  • The like-for-like comparison is the joint solve above.
  • 2-hour ctl-prior against 2-hour ctl was supplement only.

What was withdrawn or corrected along the way​

  • Spec 029's 1-hour recommendation is withdrawn. It measured "more data beats no data", and spec 031 showed that a joint solve over the same past beats it.
  • Spec 028's "narrows the permutations" (the owner's hypothesis about manoeuvre histories) is not shown either way. History counts were never measured.
  • Spec 031's tuning pointed the wrong way (D1 1.30). The panel gave a likely cause: the tuning compared runs on two CPU families, on about 34 windows.
  • Spec 032 decided only half its blind windows (85 of 165). The other 80 had lost contact. The study forecast 120 decided windows and got 85.
  • Single seeds swing.
    • Spec 032's E1 read 1.32, 0.87 and 1.54 across tuning, validation and test.
    • Joint ÷ ctl read 1.25, 1.46 and 0.72; pooled it is 0.87 [0.67, 1.11], not separated.
    • Read pooled figures, not single seeds.

What was never tested​

  • Real bearings. Every figure comes from one simulator whose realism was set by hand.
  • Bearing errors outside the generator's own family. The time-late track showed ctl knows that family, and that outliers, correlated errors and bias cost it.
  • Targets that do not follow Annex B.
  • The untended contact under a carried prior, beyond spec 028's weak split, which was mostly that contact (1.105, not shown). The untended contact is the harder case: spec 026 found it about 1.3–1.5 times worse at matched range.
  • Association between contacts, beyond spec 024's 1° swaps.
  • An ownship steered by its own estimated track, and the drift of its range belief over many hours.
  • Hand-overs inside a target manoeuvre, and a repaired, neutral-start hand-over.
  • A calibrated carried covariance.
  • Registered comparisons beyond 12 hours. The 24- and 48-hour runs were one engagement each.
  • Lost-contact windows. Neither 2-hour system answers them. The joint solve's extrapolation through them was measured (median error 0.19–0.29) but not registered.
  • Real-time requirements. Every comparison here is time-late, with up to 2 hours of latency per window.

What would justify reopening the track​

  • Measured realism: real or recorded bearings, with the detection, gaps and errors they actually have.
  • An ownship steered by its own track, or association between contacts.
  • A deployment that needs the untended contact, or answers while contact is lost. Both were outside every decided population.
  • A latency requirement that 2-hour time-late windows cannot meet.
  • A case where the bearings cannot be kept and only a hand-over can travel. There the comparison against the joint solve stops being the fair one, and the prior's compactness becomes the question.

Related work not covered here​

The joined-periods track (specs 025, 027, 030 and 033) asks how to use several short periods of one target across gaps. It uses a different layout simulator.

  • Spec 025: the joint solve stands.
  • Spec 027: a carried start does not substitute for it.
  • Spec 030: a carried prior helps where a period cannot answer alone. The joint solve stayed standing (k4 ÷ joint 1.40, on the line), and the prior's inflation was not shown to matter.
  • Spec 033 (long gaps) has a spec on main and no test record yet.
