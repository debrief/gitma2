Kickoff: leg splitters in the demonstrator

November 21, 2026 · 5 min read

In the demonstrator, the physics layer told the target's true manoeuvre times is excellent: about 1% range error where one leg gives 25–35%. Real systems are not told the times. The owner asked for effort on target leg identification, remembering Debrief's arctan slicing, and for a demo where leg splitters and TMA solvers are chosen separately so they can be compared. This is spec 036.

Where we are​

  • The demonstrator (spec 035) offers JavaScript least squares, the physics layer with one leg or with the true manoeuvre times, and spec 016's learned whole-period model.
  • The project's own leg identification:
    • C, the classical segmenter: Gate S, then Gate 2's segmenter, frozen.
    • The time-late evidence search ctl, the standing time-late system.
    • The finding of specs 013–019: much of the remaining gap is ambiguity in the bearings, not a weak search.
  • Research note 07 tried the arctan law as a sliding-window detector. Alarms used directly as knots failed. A window nominating a change and the whole history confirming it worked, and that became C.

Where this task moves to​

  • Spec specs/036-leg-splitters/: SRD §1, Annex A, and Annex B's background table.
  • Acceptance:
    • Splitters other than the truth never see the true times.
    • No false split on held-course presets.
    • On a two-turn preset the best splitter's layer solve comes close to the oracle's.
    • All of it runs in the built page.
  • Plan:
    1. Two drop-downs: leg splitter → TMA solver.
    2. Splitters:
      • Debrief's 2015 arctan slicing, as published.
      • A multi-leg slicing.
      • The arctan law with joined legs, searched over the whole window.
      • C, in the browser.
    3. A legs panel and a "run all splitters" comparison.
  • Risks:
    • C is slow in a browser (minutes).
    • Thresholds set on a few presets are not false-alarm rates.
    • Nothing here is a result.

How progress will be shown​

  • A dot chart of range error per splitter on three presets over five noise draws.
  • A track plot of the two-turn preset with each splitter's track and knots.
  • Data: scripts/research/demo_splitters.mjs on the page's own presets, recorded in experiments/20261121-demo-splitters/.

Updates​

  • 2026-11-22: from the owner's review of the merged demo:
    • An arrowhead at the end of each track shows which way it is heading.
    • A fourth optional chart, "course & speed", shows both vessels' course or speed (a drop-down: course, speed or both) with the estimate dashed. True turns are shaded (blue ownship, red target); turns a splitter detected are solid strips: orange target turns on the right edge, teal ownship turns on the left. Only Debrief's slicing detects ownship turns, so only it draws the teal strips.
    • The error chart's quantity is a drop-down, so the chart is as tall as its neighbours and their time axes line up.
    • The Preset drop-down shows the preset the scenario matches, or "custom" once edited.
    • Dragging a waypoint (circle) now keeps the adjacent legs' speeds, so their durations change. Dragging a leg's square keeps its duration, so its speed changes.
    • Ctrl-click (⌘-click) deletes a waypoint.
    • Each leg's speed label sits on the leg's north side (west for a north–south leg), clear of the square handle and the cursor that drags it. The read-out during a drag sits above and to the left of the cursor. It says what is kept and what changes: "Keeping time: speed changes" for a square (the leg's minutes, its old and new speed and course), "Keeping speed: leg times change" for a circle (each adjacent leg's speed, its old and new minutes). The hover tooltips on squares and circles say the same.
    • Schema 0.39.0: TmaSolution.splitter_ownship_spans, and the DemoSplitterInfo flags detects_target and detects_ownship.

2026-11-22: bearings lost in ownship's turns​

The owner noted that bearings are lost while ownship turns and for two minutes after each turn ends. The demonstrator now drops them by default. A scenario's turn_loss_s sets the tail (120 s); null keeps every bearing, and a checkbox under the noise controls turns the loss off. Lost bearings keep their rows, flagged in the problem's bearing_valid (schema 0.40.0), so every solver and splitter keeps its regular time grid and skips them, as Python's valid column does. Least squares, the physics layer, C, the arctan splitters and the learned model's inputs all ignore them, and the plot, bearings chart and residuals hide them. The noise is still drawn for every row, so the bearings that are kept do not change. On the good-geometry preset, 120 of 1,429 bearings are lost (three turns of 1 to 1.5 minutes, each plus two minutes).

Spec 035's golden problems and spec 036's readings and figures kept every bearing, so their scripts and tests pin turn_loss_s: null. With the loss on, joined legs on the two-turn preset places the first turn at 50 min instead of 46.5 on seed 1, outside the 120 s match. Seeds 2, 3 and 5 still find both turns. This is one draw, not a result.
