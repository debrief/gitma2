Kickoff: time-late TMA, the whole track over a given period

November 11, 2026 · 10 min read

Everything this project has scored so far is the real-time question: the best estimate now, from the bearings so far. The owner has asked for considered steps towards the other mode, time-late: given a period of bearings, the most consistent solution across the whole period. Spec 013 defines that solution and its score, gives the classical approach its best shot, and measures what is left before anything is trained.

Where we are​

  • Spec 011 closed at tuning (write-up): the classical segmenter's largest weakness is missed manoeuvres, about 30% of true changes.
  • Spec 012 (kickoff): three seeds of a direct regressor are trained. On validation it sits at 1.2 to 1.8 times the bound on held course and beats C″ after manoeuvres (0.065 against 0.105 in median relative range error). Its test reading is being scored. GPU training is paused by the owner.
  • Carried: C frozen, C″ the standing classical system, least squares at the bound when the manoeuvre times are right.
  • Never done: scoring a whole track. C″ run to the end of a window already produces one.

Where this task moves to​

  • SRD §3 (both consumers remain) and §5; a new row, Gate T, proposed for the owner to merge. Spec: specs/013-time-late/.
  • Owner's decisions (11 November): a time-late solution is judged on the whole track across the period; bearings come every 5 seconds; the analyst does not choose the period.
  • Plan:
    1. The solution and its score: position, course and speed at every moment, the manoeuvre times, and an honest "undetermined" where the data cannot say; a bound at every moment.
    2. Data: 5-second bearings, and periods cut at random from longer engagements, so a period may hold several ownship manoeuvres or none.
    3. The classical side, nothing trained: C″ read as a track; a full-period pass that re-tests every declared change with all the data and looks for missed ones; least squares told the true manoeuvre times as the reference.
    4. The owner's bearing-rate idea: thinning where the rate is steady, as dropped bearings, as fixed normal points and as adaptive bins; accuracy, run time, and wild bearings.
    5. Decision record, fresh seeds (329, 330, 331), the panel.
  • Judged by: the headroom, the best classical system's whole-track range error over the reference's. Material above 10% (proposed): the next spec trains a full-period proposer with least squares producing the answer. Not material: the classical full-period system is the time-late answer.
  • Risks: independent errors at 5 seconds overstate the information (a correlated condition is scored beside); periods with no ownship manoeuvre, where the right answer is that range is not determined; a reference that is not a ceiling for systems that do not segment.
  • We stop if the reference is not near its bound on determined rows: then the scoring is wrong, and nothing else means anything.

How progress will be shown​

  • One period's bearings and bearing-rate curve, with the compression bins drawn on it.
  • Whole-track plots: truth, C″ read as a track, the full-period system and the reference, on periods with and without an ownship manoeuvre.
  • Headroom chart with intervals, by number of ownship manoeuvres and by position in the period.
  • Misses recovered by the full period, against the causal system's.
  • Compression trade-off: accuracy against run time for each form of thinning.

Update, 11 November (evening): the reference was the weak point​

The first checkpoint tripped the plan's stop rule. The reference (the physics layer told the true manoeuvre times) sat at about twice its bound on cut periods and 4.5 times on whole engagements. The scoring was not at fault: with no target manoeuvre, least squares read as a track is at the bound. The cause, tested, is that the layer gives each manoeuvre four free unknowns where a turn at constant speed has one.

The owner chose to add a classical arc-model solve: a manoeuvre is a turn at a constant rate and a speed change at a constant rate between two times. On 60 tuning periods it reaches 1.2 times the bound told the kind of manoeuvre and 1.4 judging the kind from the data, with the true times. On C″'s own transitions it is no better than the chord and probably worse: a stricter model forgives a missed manoeuvre less. So the segmentation remains the larger factor, about three in median range error, and the full-period re-test is the next step. Records: experiments/20261111-tl-diagnosis/, experiments/20261111-tl-arc/ (tuning data, not a gate result).

Update, 11 November (late): a better search does not help​

The full-period re-test was built on the arc model: refine every manoeuvre's times, add a manoeuvre where the whole-period fit demands one, remove one it does not support. On the same 60 tuning periods it is no better than C″ read as a track (median range error 0.11 to 0.14 against 0.11; the reference told the true times is at 0.035). It fits the bearings exactly as well as the fit told the truth, with a third of the manoeuvres missed. A missed manoeuvre costs almost nothing in fit and a factor of five in range.

So the limit is not the search. Structures with different manoeuvres explain the same bearings, and what separates them is knowledge of how targets manoeuvre, which a test of fit does not use and a model trained on millions of engagements does. That is the likeliest reason spec 012's regressor beat C″. Record: experiments/20261111-tl-retest/ (tuning data).

Update, 12 November: prior knowledge does not pick the history either​

The owner chose to try a classical estimator that uses Annex B's manoeuvre statistics. The cheap test came first: under those statistics, is the true manoeuvre history more probable than the wrong one the re-test finds? It is not. Where the re-test is badly wrong, the truth is clearly ahead in about four periods in ten and the wrong history clearly ahead in about four in ten. The true history fits slightly better and pays more than that for its extra manoeuvres. The re-test was already close to the most probable history.

Told only the right history, with every time left free, the fit sits at the bound. So the problem is neither the solve nor the timing: in about half the difficult periods the bearings and the statistics together leave two histories standing, and they put the target in different places. An honest system would say so, with candidate tracks and probabilities. Record: experiments/20261112-tl-prior/ (tuning data).

Correction, 12 November (later): the prior does help​

The update above is wrong in its main claim and is withdrawn. The comparison had scored each history's plausibility at a fit to the bearings alone, where a loosely determined manoeuvre duration often lands on an impossible turn rate; the true history was then charged thousands of units for a rate it does not have. The slides' histogram showed it at once.

With the manoeuvre statistics inside the fit, the picture changes. Where the re-test is wrong, the true history is clearly the more probable in half or more of the periods and the wrong one in fewer than one in ten. And the re-test's own history, refitted with the statistics and no truth, is at about half the error of the standing classical system read as a track (0.057 against 0.113 in median range error; ratio 1.99, interval 1.01 to 3.11, on 57 tuning periods). About a third of the difficult periods remain genuinely ambiguous. The direct next step is a classical search over histories scored by that evidence. Record: experiments/20261112-tl-prior/ (the withdrawn files are kept beside the corrected ones).

Update, 12 November (evening): a classical search, twice as good as the standing system​

The owner chose to continue classically. A search over manoeuvre histories, scored by how probable each is given the bearings and Annex B's statistics, was run once on 99 tuning periods it had not seen during development. Its median range error over the whole track is 0.042 against 0.088 for the standing classical system read as a track (ratio 2.1, interval 1.6 to 3.0). It does as well started from nothing as started from the standing system's answer. The fit told the true history is at 0.018.

The search is no longer the weak point: where it is wrong it has usually found a history that scores above the true one. What limits it is the score, and periods that really do admit two explanations. The probabilities it attaches to its candidate tracks are not yet trustworthy. Next comes a gate on fresh seeds, with a test split whose targets manoeuvre differently from what the search assumes. Record: experiments/20261112-tl-search/ (tuning data).

Update, 12 November (midday): the gate stopped at its first check​

Gate T was registered and its validation seed run. The search did what the tuning seed promised: about two and a half times better than the standing classical system read as a track, and 1.7 times better on targets that manoeuvre otherwise than it assumes. But the gate's first check, on the soundness of its own reference, failed, and by the rule written beforehand the gate stops and the test seed is not generated.

The reference was a fit told the true manoeuvre history. It scored better than the theoretical bound allows, which is a fault, not a success. The fit had been started at the truth, and where the bearings pin the target down only weakly it simply stayed there. On well-determined rows it is exactly at the bound; on the rest it is the truth handed back. The check that caught it was registered with a forecast of "pass, 0.90".

Two other things the validation run showed. The search's candidate probabilities are honest when its assumptions about manoeuvres are right (it names the true history first in 61% of periods and claims 54%), and badly overconfident when they are wrong (22% against 52%). Record: experiments/20261112-gt-val/. What happens next is the owner's decision.

Update, 12 November (evening): the gate is decided​

The owner chose to register the gate again with a reference fitted from neutral starts and every threshold unchanged. On a new validation seed the soundness check passed, and the test seed was run: five of six checks passed and the sixth failed as forecast. The review panel approved with findings and narrowed most of the first interpretation. The search halves the median track error of the standing classical system, mostly by smoothing with a better model and the manoeuvre statistics rather than by finding histories; it under-counts manoeuvres; and its probabilities are honest only on average and only when its assumptions hold. Several studies this spec promised, the bearing-rate thinning idea among them, were not run and are owed. The write-up is Gate T, with slides.
