Gate 3 asked whether the carried Gate 2 estimator E can state an uncertainty that can be trusted.
The answer on validation is no, and the gate stopped there: the calibration did not meet the
pre-registered validation rule, so the fresh test seed was not generated.

E's raw covariance, from the physics solve, is badly overconfident: its 95% interval covers 0.60 of
rows in log range and only a third of rows fall within the χ²₄ 95% point. A learned calibration
can widen the stated standard deviations until 92–99% of each quantity is covered. It cannot make
the joint 4-state statement honest (0.835 within the χ²₄ point, against 0.92 required), and it does
no better than a classical per-slice temperature. The review panel found why, and the reasons
point to a different design.

## What was tested

Every prediction row now states the covariance of (x, y, vx, vy) at the row and the standard
deviations of log range, course and speed derived from it (see
[uncertainty calibration](/algorithms/calibration)). On a validation baseline before any training,
every estimator was overconfident except the oracle:

| | 95% coverage, log range | Within χ²₄ 95% | Median NEES ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E | 0.60 | 0.33 | 6.7 |
| C | 0.61 | 0.36 | 7.3 |
| Oracle (true segmentation) | 0.875 | 0.75 | 1.32 |
| IMM | 0.41 (22% no estimate) | 0.37 | 10.6 |

A calibration head rescales E's covariance without touching its points. It reads E's encoder state
at the row, the raw standard deviations and C's code, and outputs four log-scales, for the standard
deviations of log range, bearing, speed and course, keeping the stated correlations. It was trained
on E's frozen features over the training stream, three seeds and a shuffled-label control. The
classical control is a temperature: the same four scales fitted per cell of (time since the zig ×
transition open at the row).

The [decision record](https://github.com/debrief/gitma2/tree/main/experiments/20261108-g3-decision)
fixed the criteria before training. Two changes came after the baseline and before any training.
The owner replaced the original consistency check (median NEES near its χ²₄ value) with a tail
check, because E's errors are heavy-tailed and the two original checks could not both pass. And
the head's form moved from one scale to four local ones, because covering course needed its
standard deviation widened about 30 times, range and speed only about 5. The decision forecast an
overall pass at about 0.1, with the tail check the likely failure.

## The result (validation, 3,934 rows)

| Check | Carried head | Band | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G3-a 95% coverage, log range / course / speed | 0.986 / 0.923 / 0.984 | 0.92–0.98 | inconclusive |
| G3-b share within the χ²₄ 95% point | 0.835 [0.819, 0.849] | 0.92–0.98 | **fail** |
| G3-c head − temperature, interval score in log range | +1.07 [+0.98, +1.17] | below 0 | **fail** |
| G3-d points unchanged | exact | — | pass |

## Why, according to the panel

Five reviewers (statistics, numerics, TMA geometry, reproducibility, red team) approved the stop and
corrected the account:

- **Course coverage is largely vacuous.** For more than half the carried head's rows the 95% course
  interval covers the whole circle (median stated course sd 101°). Course coverage is reached by
  giving up on course, not by calibrating it. Course binds because it is the least observable state
  on short or low-bearing-rate legs and just after a zig.
- **The kept correlations sink the joint check.** E's local covariance is near-singular (median
  correlation 0.81 between log range and speed). With the same scales and the correlations
  dropped, 0.957 of rows would fall within the χ²₄ point. A scale-only family cannot pass.
- **The loss is not the score.** The head wins on its training loss and loses on the interval
  score: it widens log range everywhere to absorb tails that course drives. It trained on
  linearised errors, which cannot see a course flip, while the gate scores exact errors.
- **No per-row learning is shown.** The head is close to a constant; moved by its best single
  factor, it is no better than its shuffled control.
- **Much of the failure is physical.** On rows during or just after a manoeuvre even the oracle,
  with the true segmentation, falls well short (0.64 within the χ²₄ point): the bearings have not
  yet determined the new leg.

## What follows

Recommended follow-on (adopted if the owner has not objected by the next spec):

1. **Report course as undetermined when it is.** A circular distribution for course, or an explicit
   "course not yet determined" state, instead of a Gaussian sd above 90°.
2. **Gate the uncertainty on observability.** Calibrate and report on rows where the leg is
   determined, using a causal proxy (time since the last detected change, C's open-transition
   code), with a "manoeuvre suspected" flag otherwise.
3. **Shrink the stated correlations** and **train on the exact errors** the gate scores.
4. A heavy-tailed predictive only for the manoeuvre component, weighted by E's own change evidence.

The carried estimator stays E; its point estimates are unchanged. Its raw covariance should not be
shown to an operator as an uncertainty. The validation record, the decision and the panel's
reports are in
[experiments/20261108-g3-val](https://github.com/debrief/gitma2/tree/main/experiments/20261108-g3-val).
