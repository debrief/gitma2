title: "Gate 4: a course statement that is honest on average, but not yet where it matters"

Gate 4 asked whether the carried Gate 2 estimator E can state an uncertainty an operator can act
on. The course statement is "within ±X with probability P, otherwise unknown", and range and speed
get 95% intervals. On validation the statement is honest on average. A learned head beats the
pre-registered classical control. But one per-bin reliability check missed its band, so by the
rule fixed beforehand the gate stopped at validation and the fresh test seed (320) was not
generated.

The review panel upheld the stop unanimously. It also found that the record's first reading was
too generous on three points:
- the miss was not a near-miss of one unlucky seed;
- the head's gain is mostly a classical function of E's own standard deviations;
- P is reliable on average but not on the rows just after a manoeuvre.

## What was tested

Gate 3 showed that a Gaussian interval on E's course can only be honest at 95% by covering the
whole circle on half the rows: E's course errors are heavy-tailed (35% over 30°) and mostly not
predictable from causal signals. Gate 4 (spec 009, `specs/009-usable-uncertainty/`) states
uncertainty differently:

- **Course:** a mixture, a von Mises around E's course plus a uniform. The row states an arc ±X
  (X at most 45°) and the probability P that the true course lies inside it. P is mapped through
  an isotonic recalibration fitted on validation tuning rows.
- **Range and speed:** 95% half-widths c·sd, trained on the interval score.
- **The learned head:** a small network on E's cached features (its encoder state, its sds, C's
  code) gives the four scales per row, trained on the exact errors.
- **The classical control:** a per-cell temperature (minutes since the zig × open transition). It
  is stated on sd^β, with β chosen per quantity on tuning rows. With plain sd the shuffled head
  beat it in sample, so the redesign made the control stronger.
- **The criteria** (SRD amendment, owner to merge; decision record
  `experiments/20261108-g4-decision/`):
  - **G4-a:** range and speed coverage in [0.92, 0.98];
  - **G4-b:** hit rate minus P within ±0.03 overall and ±0.05 in each of five bins of P;
  - **G4-c:** a course log score better than "unknown", and mean P of at least 0.5;
  - **G4-d:** the head beats the temperature, unless a shuffled head also does;
  - **G4-e:** points unchanged.

## The result (validation, 3,934 rows)

The carried seed, st-s3, is the median of three by the registered rule.

| Check | Value [97.5% interval] | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G4-a range coverage | 0.940 [0.926, 0.950] | pass |
| G4-a speed coverage | 0.929 [0.915, 0.941] | inconclusive |
| G4-b overall hit − P | −0.002 [−0.024, +0.021] | pass |
| G4-b top bin of P | stated 0.675, hit 0.729 | **fail** (limit 0.05) |
| G4-c course log gain over "unknown" | +1.01 [0.92, 1.10]; mean P 0.62 | pass |
| G4-d head − temperature | −0.235 [−0.355, −0.137] | pass |
| G4-e points | identical | pass |

For contrast, E's raw sds claim P 0.90 for a ±17° arc and are right on 0.51 of rows. Their course
statement is 2.6 nats per row worse than saying "unknown".

## What the panel found

- **The miss is real, and the check was fragile.** Under resampling, some bin misses ±0.05 in 87%
  of draws for the carried seed, and in 43–47% for the other two. The top bin is under-stated for
  every seed. The per-bin band is about 2.5 sampling standard deviations wide, so a perfectly
  calibrated statement would still miss it a quarter of the time. That is a design flaw in G4-b,
  and it is fixed in the next spec rather than here.
- **The isotonic map caused it.** In the top bin the raw head is, if anything, over-confident:
  it states 0.76 against a hit rate of 0.73. The 10-knot map, fitted on 1,971 tuning rows, pooled
  its top two knots and capped P at 0.677.
- **P is reliable on average, not where it matters.**
  - On rows still settling after a target manoeuvre, a quarter of all rows, P is 0.62 and the hit
    rate 0.30. On settled rows it is 0.63 against 0.73.
  - P barely varies across rows (0.46–0.68).
  - Rows whose course is more than 90° off still state P ≈ 0.62.
  - The temperature behaves the same way.
- **The learning is mostly a classical rule.** The head beats the registered temperature in
  course, range and speed alike. But a classical rule fitted on the tuning rows alone recovers 72%
  of that margin: each scale is quadratic in E's three log sds, 43 parameters in all. The shuffled
  control could not show this, because it keeps the sds while it shuffles the errors. The residual
  0.07 is unattributed.
- **The course statement contradicts itself.** P is recalibrated, but the density (scored by the
  log score) is not, so the two disagree by up to 0.2 on a row. The normal approximation to the
  von Mises adds up to 0.12.
- **The intervals are honest but wide.** The 95% range interval spans a factor of 1.74 either way
  at the median, so at 20 km it runs 11–35 km at the 95th percentile. The speed half-width is
  about 6.7 kn. Coverage falls short at minute 5 and beyond 30 km.

The full record is in `experiments/20261109-g4-val/`, with the panel's findings in its
`review.md`.

## What follows

Recommended default for spec 010, adopted unless the owner objects:
- Keep the statement's shape (arc plus probability, interval scores). Make it one probability:
  use the exact von Mises mass, recalibrate the density too, and use a smoother recalibration.
- Gate reliability by slice, including minutes 1–5, open transitions and range bands. Add a
  causal "unsettled" proxy that lowers P after a suspected manoeuvre.
- Replace the per-bin point band with an interval or calibration-slope test.
- Register two controls before any result exists: a cross-sd classical rule fitted on the
  training stream, and a shuffled control that permutes only E's encoder state.

Seed 320 stays unused.
