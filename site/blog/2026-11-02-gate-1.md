title: "Gate 1: the physics layer survives training, and the network learns nothing"

Gate 1 passed on constant-velocity data, but not for the reason the project hoped. The learned
heads collapsed to constants during training, so the carried model *is* batch least squares
computed by the physics layer. What Gate 1 shows is narrower and still worth having: end-to-end
training through the declared physics layer is stable and does not degrade least squares at the
information limit. Whether the learned component can add anything is untested until Gate 2.

## The gate

On a fresh test seed (303, 10,000 scenarios, generated once), σ = 1° rows at zig + 5 min (range,
course) and zig + 10 min (speed), the carried model had to be within 1.1 × batch least squares on
every median with the whole interval below, have a gross-error rate at or below least squares',
and have efficiency (median error ÷ Cramér–Rao bound ÷ 0.6745) at most 1.25. All of it was fixed in
a decision record committed before training (`experiments/20261101-g1-decision/`).

| Check | Result | 95% interval | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Range, ratio to least squares | 0.999 | 0.996–1.002 | < 1.1 |
| Course, ratio | 0.997 | 0.994–1.000 | < 1.1 |
| Speed, ratio | 1.000 | 1.000–1.000 (identity) | < 1.1 |
| Gross errors, model − least squares | −0.08 points (58 vs 62 of 4,884 rows) | −0.16 to −0.02 | ≤ 0 |
| Efficiency, range / course / speed | 0.99 / 0.96 / 0.96 | upper bounds 1.03 / 1.00 / 1.00 | ≤ 1.25 |

Beside it, on the same rows: least squares 3.33% median range error, the tuned IMM 1.03 ×
least squares and the EKF 1.04 ×, naive about 12 ×. On constant-velocity data least squares is at
the bound and the trackers' process noise costs them a little, as expected.

## What the panel found

Five independent reviewers (geometry, numerics, statistics, reproducibility, red team) approved
with findings, and every one of the substantive findings points the same way:

- **The heads are dead.** In the carried model every head ends with 0 of 64 hidden units active;
  the per-bearing weights are one constant, the start correction and covariance scale are their
  biases. The trained model's estimate equals an untrained one's to about 1e-10. Its test numbers
  equal, to every digit, those of the physics layer with trivial inputs (the "oracle" row, which on
  constant-velocity data has nothing to know). An untrained model would have passed.
- **The gross-error edge is the solver's.** Four rows went the model's way and none the other
  (exact sign test p = 0.06); the untrained layer has the same four. They come from the layer's
  cold-start solve with a damping ladder against the warm-started per-timestep least squares port,
  and from how each defines convergence, not from a learned start. The decision record's forecast
  said the learned start would avoid wrong minima; that was wrong.
- **The validation stop that was not.** The pre-registered shuffled-label control had to score
  "at naive level". Run through the physics layer it scores near least squares whatever it learns,
  so the registered rule would have stopped the phase at validation. The owner amended the rule
  (constitution 1.2.0) after training started and before the validation report; the amendment and
  this consequence are recorded.

None of this changes the verdict under the registered rules, and all of it changes what the
verdict means.

## Why the heads died

On constant-velocity data with Gaussian noise, uniform weights and the least squares start are
optimal, so the loss gives the heads nothing to do; the untrained model's validation loss (3.27 per
row, against 3.0 at the bound) is already at the floor, and it stayed there for every seed. The
heads' last layers start at zero by design, so the model starts as least squares; with nothing to
gain, the hidden ReLU layers drifted dead and the encoder's output collapsed. That is a legitimate
optimum here and a problem for Gate 2: a dead ReLU never revives.

## What changes before Gate 2

- No warm start from these weights; head activations that cannot die; a test of alive units and of
  how much head outputs vary across rows.
- A learnability check before any Gate 2 plan, on data where the heads must matter (outlier or
  heteroscedastic bearings, or the manoeuvring data), so a dead network cannot pass silently again.
- Weights bound by digest in predictions and gate records; gross errors reported by cause; one
  median convention for efficiency; a minimum improvement in early stopping.

## Caveats

- Zig + 5 min falls inside the second ownship turn for about two thirds of the rows; these numbers
  are not comparable with gitma's single-zig results.
- Efficiency is median-based; least squares itself scores below 1 on course and speed, which
  reflects heavy tails rather than beating the bound, so the 1.25 limit separates little this close
  to it.
- The direct-regression control was under-trained (validation loss 50.5 → 50.0); its 7–12 × is
  not a fair measure of direct regression.
