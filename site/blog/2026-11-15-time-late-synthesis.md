Between 11 and 15 November the project ran six studies on the time-late consumer: the
analyst who wants the target's whole track over a period after the fact, not the best
estimate now. Each has its own write-up and its own panel review. This is the account of
all six together: what is standing, what was shown, what was withdrawn, and what was never
tested. Figures are from the committed test records and their panel reviews; the table of
record is generated by a script. The illustrated companion is
[the slides post](/blog/2026/11/15/time-late-synthesis-slides).

<!-- truncate -->

## The question

Given every bearing of a period of 40 to 120 minutes, at 5-second intervals, say where the
target was, its course and its speed at every moment, and when it manoeuvred. Nothing was
trained except in one study, which says so.

## What is standing

**The classical control** (study L's `ctl`, declared):

1. wild bearings are edited out;
2. the bearing sd is enlarged when the bearings' second differences show the errors are
   correlated in time;
3. a search over manoeuvre histories, each fitted with an arc model (a turn at a constant
   rate, a speed change at a constant rate) and scored by its evidence under Annex B's
   manoeuvre statistics and under a broadened set, pooled;
4. a manoeuvre is declared only when it is worth an evidence margin, with a stated 1%
   chance that the target never manoeuvres.

It replaced "C″ read as a track": the causal segmenter's answer at the end of the period,
read backwards.

## What was shown

All ratios are paired per period, in period median range error, on fresh test seeds.

**The search with the arc model and Annex B's statistics halves the old system's error.**
C″ ÷ search: 2.05 and 2.24 on Annex B targets (two seeds), 1.64 and 1.65 on busier
targets, 3.1 with 2% of bearings wild. The panels narrowed what that means. It is mostly
smoothing with the right motion model and prior, not finding the right history: the
search wins where its history is wrong too, and refitting C″'s own manoeuvres with the arc
model and prior obtains between a third and a half of the gain (specs 014 and 015). A
search under a flat prior obtains about four fifths of it: Annex B's statistics themselves
are worth about a fifth.

**Editing and an honest bearing sd remove most of the damage from bad bearings.** Search ÷
control: 1.68 with 2% wild bearings, 2.44 with 5%, 1.33 with correlated errors, 1.70 on
targets that hold course (false manoeuvres in 8% of periods against 49%). Replicated on
three seeds. On clean Annex B targets the control is not separated from the search (1.05
to 1.09), and under a slowly varying bias it does nothing (1.00).

**Against the old system the standing control is 2.1 to 2.4 times better on Annex B
targets, 1.6 to 1.7 on busier targets, about 5 with wild bearings, 2.9 with correlated
errors, and level on targets that hold course** (seeds 344 and 347).

**What remains, against a fit told the true manoeuvre history.** 1.3 on Annex B targets,
1.2 on targets with larger, rarer manoeuvres, 1.8 to 2.1 on busier targets (three seeds).
These references are told true times and are not bounds: the control beats them in a
quarter of periods.

**On busier targets the remainder is mostly not closable.** Over a third of their
manoeuvres add less than 20 to the fit's χ² when left out (16% on Annex B targets). Where
a manoeuvre is hidden the control is within 1.2 of a reference that is not told it. The
closable part sits in the under-a-third of periods where the control misses a manoeuvre
the bearings show (spec 017).

## What was tried against the control, and lost

| Challenger | Annex B targets | Busier targets | Study |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learned whole-period model, its declared read-out (three seeds, 12.5 million periods each) | 0.81 to 0.82 | 0.80 to 0.82 | 016 |
| The same model's own track | 0.71 | 1.01 in range; 0.79 course, 0.77 speed | 016 |
| Search told the targets' true statistics | 0.96, 0.98 | 1.17, 1.10; 1.15 [1.07, 1.23] over three seeds | 017, 018 |
| A prior chosen from 30 by the period's evidence | 0.93; 0.91 [0.84, 0.98] over three seeds | 1.00 [0.92, 1.08] | 018 |
| Hedged mean of candidate tracks | 0.92 | 1.00 in range, about 0.9 in course | 018 |

Control ÷ challenger; above 1 the challenger is better.

- **The learned model lost in the conditions it was trained on.** The panel showed the
  registered comparison measured its untuned manoeuvre detector: the median period is a
  tie, and the loss sits where the model declares fewer manoeuvres. Its own track leans on
  what targets do in general, winning where the bearings say little and losing by three
  where they say much. "This model with this read-out stops"; a learned proposer inside
  the search was not tested.
- **The right prior is worth about 15% on busier targets, in a tail.** Ten periods of 355
  carry most of it, and it loses by a factor of two in 13% of periods.
- **One period cannot choose its prior.** Its evidence does not rank a family of priors by
  their accuracy; choosing by groups of periods does no better.
- **A hedged mean is not better than a declared history**, and its spread could not be
  stated by one factor.

## The last diagnostic

Spec 018's panel left one thing confounded: the family of priors only refitted a shared
set of candidate histories, and it lacked parts of the true statistics. One run on 130
periods already used (seed 347; development work, not a result) separates the two.

- **Refitting was not the handicap.** The family member nearest the truth, given a search
  of its own, is 1.03 [0.91, 1.19] of the control; refitting shared candidates on the same
  periods it was 1.03 [0.91, 1.19].
- **No single part of the true prior buys its gain.** Taking on the truth's legs, turn
  sizes, turn rates, speed changes and kinds of manoeuvre one at a time moves the ratio
  from 0.98 to 1.11 [0.94, 1.32] by steps of a few per cent. Each part alone on the
  broadened prior does nothing or costs a little (0.92 to 1.00).
- **The gain is a tail at every step.** Without its ten best periods every one of them,
  the true prior included (0.94), is behind the control.
- **What the right prior changes steadily is the count**: manoeuvres missed fall from 0.52
  to 0.36, with false ones near a half a period throughout. In course and speed it is
  further ahead (1.21 and 1.24, not quite separated).

So the right prior is worth having only whole, it cannot be assembled from one or two
corrected statistics, and one period cannot say which whole prior is right.

## What was withdrawn or corrected along the way

The record matters as much as the results. In six studies:

- **Spec 013's first reading of the manoeuvre prior was withdrawn**: the evidence had been
  evaluated at a bearings-only fit, which produced thousands of units of artefact. The
  owner's decision made on that reading went back to the owner.
- **Gate T stopped at validation the first time**: its true-history reference started at
  the truth and stayed there, scoring better than the bound. It was re-registered with a
  reference fitted from neutral starts.
- **The evidence had its Laplace volumes wrong four times** (a duration's volume, a
  manoeuvre cut by the period's edge, the edges of the prior's support). Two were repaired
  in spec 015; the last is recorded and unrepaired, and applies to every system since.
- **Forecasts that were wrong**: that the scaled regressor would not beat C″ (012, before
  this track); spec 014's share of the search's gain from refitting (0.79 forecast, 0.43
  and 0.33 measured); spec 017's that the control would not be separated from the
  detectable-only reference; spec 018's of a trade. Forecasts from one tuning seed of
  about 130 periods were guesses each time.
- **Rules that measured the wrong thing**: spec 016's comparison (a detector, not a
  model); spec 017's second condition (counts, not cost); spec 018's spread band (its edge
  sat on the tuning value).
- **Words the panels took back**: "ceiling", "bound", "upper limit" and "lower limit" for
  fits told the truth; "ambiguous" for a property of the Laplace evidence; "harm" for a
  tail with a level median.

## What was never tested

- A learned proposer inside the search, or training through the arc model.
- A longer history of the same target than one period, to learn its habits.
- Advice to ownship: where the bearings do not show a manoeuvre, ownship's own motion is
  what would.
- A bias in the bearings that varies slowly: nothing built addresses it.
- A whitened fit or a Huber cost in place of editing and an enlarged sd; the evidence's
  repair at the edges of the prior's support.
- A smoothed IMM as a reference; a spread for course and speed; real data of any kind.
- Whole engagements beyond 60 scored once, and periods under 40 minutes.

## What it adds to the project's question

The project asks whether a learned component can match the classical solution where the
simple motion model holds and beat it where it does not. On the time-late consumer the
answer so far is no, for one small direct model at one budget, and the reason is
informative. Every large gain came from stating a classical piece of knowledge exactly:
how a manoeuvre unfolds, how often targets manoeuvre, what a wild bearing looks like. What
is left on targets unlike Annex B's is mostly manoeuvres the bearings do not show, and the
rest resisted a learned model and two classical ones.

A learned approach would be worth trying again if one of three things changed: the data
(real bearings, whose errors and target habits no simulator prior states exactly); the
information (several periods of the same target, or ownship manoeuvres chosen to reveal
it); or the role (a proposer whose suggestions the evidence still judges). None is in the
SRD's present scope without the owner's decision.
