---
title: "Gate D: the steady-leg detector stops at validation"
date: 2026-11-02

The learned steady-leg detector was meant to tell the physics layer where the target's legs begin
and end. It did not earn a test. On validation, its manoeuvre probability never reached the
pre-registered threshold. Its downstream estimate beat batch least squares only slightly and lost
to the tuned IMM. So the pre-registered rule stopped the phase before the test split was
generated.

The agent review panel confirmed the stop after two rounds. It found no pipeline error and
corrected our first explanation of why.

## What was tested

SRD Annex A asks for a detector with three outputs, which the [physics layer](/algorithms/physics-layer)
consumes as its knots and speed prior:

- whether a manoeuvre is happening now;
- where the changepoints are;
- whether each change is in course or speed.

The [detector](/algorithms/detector) shares the Gate 1 encoder. It was trained on its labels
and on the downstream error of the solve it feeds, through the physics layer.

Before training, the [decision record](https://github.com/debrief/gitma2/tree/main/experiments/20261102-gd-decision)
fixed:

- the threshold, 0.5;
- the tolerance, ±60 s;
- the controls (CUSUM, a windowed GLR, the IMM's mode probability);
- the checks;
- a validation rule: a variant goes to test only if every gated check's point already meets its
  limit on validation.

The plan trained seven detectors on free runners: three seeds each of a causal and a time-late
variant, and a classification-only control.

## What happened

On validation seed 304, σ = 1° G5 rows:

| Estimator | Median range error, all G5 rows | Settled rows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| batch least squares | 12.1% | 6.8% |
| IMM (frozen) | 9.5% | 7.5% |
| causal detector → physics layer | 11.5% | 6.6% |
| time-late detector → physics layer | 26.8% | 16.0% |
| oracle (true manoeuvre times) | 5.2% | 4.0% |

The causal detector improved on least squares by 5% and recovered 9% of the gap to the oracle. It
was 1.21× the IMM, with interval [1.15, 1.27], so D-downstream-all failed. It raised no alarm at
all: 0 of 8,943 manoeuvres detected and 0 false alarms. So D-delay failed too. On settled rows it
was slightly better than least squares, which passed.

The controls were tuned to the detector's own 0% false-alarm rate. That silenced them as well, so
the delay and miss comparisons set silent against silent.

The time-late variant reads the whole window. With almost no knots it fits one leg across the
later manoeuvres, so it does much worse than least squares.

## Why: a learning failure, not proof that manoeuvres are invisible

Our first write-up said the detector "learned the base rate". The panel showed that this was
half right.

- **Its "now" probability ranks rows by time alone.** It is near zero before the zig, where no
  manoeuvre can start, and flat afterwards. After the zig its AUC is about 0.5. It does not
  respond to how strongly a manoeuvre bends the bearings, at σ = 1° or 2°.
- **The time-late variant does no better.** It can see the bearings after each turn, and it is
  still at chance.
- **The manoeuvres are identifiable in hindsight.** The oracle halves least squares' error, and
  the IMM's own mode probability detects about half the manoeuvres at a 5% false-alarm rate.

So the encoder did not learn to tell target turns from ownship turns. Three design choices made
that worse:

- **The downstream loss swamped the classification losses.** It took the shared encoder's
  gradient about 1000:1.
- **The gate scored hard knots.** Training optimised soft changepoint weights, but the gate scored
  only knots exported at w ≥ 0.5, which threw away about half the learned gain.
- **The labels made 0.5 unreachable.** A 60-second "now" label, held legs weighted ×3, and ±60 s
  triangular targets meant the threshold could only be met with near-certain timing.

## What follows

Annex A §A.6 fixed the follow-on in advance. Gate D failed on more than false alarms, so the
result is published here, and Gate 2 proceeds with the CUSUM/GLR segmenter.

The next spec declares that segmenter's operating point. At a 5% held-leg false-alarm rate the
GLR detected 57% of manoeuvres on validation, against 10% for the least-squares CUSUM.

We recommend the GLR at 5%, and a lesson for any future detector gate: a floor on the matched
false-alarm rate, so that a silent detector cannot make the comparisons degenerate. Gate 2's
end-to-end model may still learn segmentation through the estimation loss. This result says it
will not come for free.
