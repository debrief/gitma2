Gate 5 asked again for a course statement an operator can trust: "within ±X with probability P,
otherwise unknown". This time the statement had to be self-consistent, P had to drop after a
suspected manoeuvre, and the learned part was tested against the classical rules it might be.

On validation the gate stopped. The learning check was void: a head with its encoder input
shuffled also beat the classical control. So the fresh test seed (320) was not generated.

The statement is now consistent: the probability it states for the arc is exactly the arc's mass
under its own distribution. P varies from row to row and ranks hits. The review panel also found:
- P is still over-stated on most rows where the target has just manoeuvred;
- the course arc is fitted too narrow;
- an encoder-free model beats the learned head.

## What was tested

Spec 010 (`specs/010-honest-course-statement/`) followed the Gate 4 write-up. On the tuning rows it
found:
- **The normal approximation was unusable.** The P of the old mixture statement differed from the
  exact von Mises mass by up to 0.5 on broad rows.
- **C's segmentation barely sees unsettled rows.** As a signal for rows just after a target
  manoeuvre its AUC was 0.55. Recent bearing residuals do better: a combined, held-out AUC of about
  0.65.

The design:
- **The truncated form.** The row states X, P and a concentration κ. Inside the arc the density is
  P times a von Mises truncated to ±X; outside, the remaining 1 − P is uniform. The arc mass is P by
  construction.
- **P.** A logistic function of E's three standard deviations, a causal "unsettled" proxy (recent
  bearing residuals and C's open flag) and the minute. A two-parameter Platt map, fitted on tuning
  rows, comes last.
- **The statements compared.** The classical rule (quadratics in the log sds, plus linear proxy
  terms) and the per-cell temperature are the controls. The learned head is the classical rule plus
  a bounded residual on E-w's encoder state. An encoder-only shuffle is the void rule.
- **The checks are gated per slice, with intervals:** minute, open transition, range band and the
  proxy's two states. Gate 5's bands were first tested on synthetic statements, and loosened when
  the first proposal passed a calibrated statement only 60% of the time.

## The result (validation, 3,934 rows; carried seed st5-s1)

| Check | Value [97.5% interval] | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G5-a coverage | range 0.938, speed 0.938; no slice wholly outside | pass |
| G5-b reliability | overall −0.006; slope 1.25 [1.06, 1.47]; every slice not wholly outside ±0.05 | inconclusive |
| G5-c usefulness | log gain +0.80; AUC 0.654 | pass |
| G5-d learning | head − classical −0.081; encoder shuffle − classical −0.039 [−0.075, −0.005] | **void** |
| G5-e consistency | points identical; mass error 6e-8 | pass |

Per the decision record, only G5-d failed, so the classical rule's statement is carried.

## What the panel found

All five reviewers upheld the stop. Their findings correct much of the first reading:

- **P is honest on average, not where it matters.**
  - The rows the proxy flags are mildly over-confident: −0.054 for the carried classical rule, with
    an interval excluding 0.
  - The flag catches only 42% of the rows just after a target manoeuvre, and the 572 rows it misses
    state 0.53 and hit 0.22.
  - The statement has to carry a caveat: P assumes no undetected target manoeuvre in the last 5
    minutes.
  - The flag also reacts to geometry: close range, and residuals after ownship's own turn.
- **The slope problem is the recalibration's.** Without the Platt map the classical rule's P has a
  slope of 1.06. The map, fitted on 1,951 tuning rows, had b = 0.85 [0.67, 1.05], and the mapped
  slope is the unmapped one divided by b: 1.06 / 0.85 ≈ 1.25. The map made a nearly calibrated P
  worse.
- **The course arc is fitted too narrow.** The arc's width is tied to the spread, and the log
  score jumps where the error crosses X, a jump that gradient fits cannot see. The temperature,
  which searched its width on a grid, has the best course score of all (by 0.12 nats). The head's
  whole margin is in the range and speed intervals.
- **No learning from E-w's state beyond an encoder-free model is shown.** The encoder helps a
  little inside the bounded head (−0.041 against its shuffle). But a plain MLP without the encoder,
  trained after the result by the red team, beats the classical rule by 0.16 and the head by 0.08.
  So the head underfits a nonlinear use of the classical features.
- **The carried statement is not the best one available.** It is under-dispersed through its Platt
  map, under-confident at 2–5 km (+0.12), and dominated by the encoder-free MLP.
- **The per-slice check was weaker than its synthetic check suggested.** The synthetic statements
  had too wide a spread of P and no within-scenario correlation. With the real structure, a slice
  off by 0.08 passes about half the time.
- **The intervals are honest but wide.** The 95% range interval spans a factor of 1.74 either way
  at the median, and the speed half-width is about ±7 kt.

The full record is in `experiments/20261109-g5-val/` (`README.md`, `review.md`).

## What follows

Recommended default for spec 011, adopted unless the owner objects:

1. Fit the course arc without gradients, by grid or line search on its width.
2. Drop the Platt map, or fit it with an interval on separate tuning scenarios.
3. Register an encoder-free MLP and an unbounded head with its encoder shuffled as controls,
   before any result.
4. Recalibrate the per-slice check on synthetic data that reproduces the real P spread and cluster
   correlation, with power against 0.08 shortfalls.
5. Improve the manoeuvre signal: separate ownship geometry from target manoeuvres, and stratify by
   ownship manoeuvre and range (Annex B).
6. Give the oracle a statement that uses the manoeuvre times it knows, as a fair ceiling.
7. Write the stated files and proxy caches on CI, with digests in their headers.

Seed 320 stays unused.
